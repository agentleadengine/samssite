Warming tools

4 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

My take: Treat mailbox warming as an optional test, not proof that a mailbox looks legitimate or that a campaign will land. Compare matched mailboxes with and without the tool under the same sending conditions before paying for it.

What warming tools do (recap)

Covered in more detail on inbox warming:

  1. Send small amounts of email from your mailbox to other warmed mailboxes
  2. Have the recipients auto-reply
  3. Move spam to inbox where needed
  4. Mark emails as "not spam" automatically
  5. Gradually increase volume over weeks

Whether these automated exchanges improve inbox placement is a hypothesis, not a guaranteed reputation gain. Compare matched mailboxes with and without warming. Hold cold-send volume, audience, copy, and authentication fixed. Review seed placement and provider reputation after a fixed trial window.

My takeverify with a controlled trial

Bundled vs standalone

Bundled with cold email tools

Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist all include warming in the main product. One account, one dashboard, warming happens automatically alongside your campaigns.

Pros:

  • One tool, one bill
  • Warming data integrated with campaign performance
  • Easy to enable per mailbox

Cons:

  • Quality depends on the tool's warming network
  • Less control over warming behavior

Standalone warming tools

Mailwarm, Warmy, MailReach, TrulyInbox. Dedicated to warming only.

Pros:

  • Often larger, more diverse warming networks
  • More advanced features (custom message templates, bigger volume ramps)
  • Can use with any sending tool

Cons:

  • Extra subscription
  • Separate dashboard

Tool comparison

Instantly (built-in)

Do not assume plan scope or placement quality. Confirm both before connecting production mailboxes.

Smartlead (built-in)

Comparable to Instantly's warming. Similar quality.

Lemlist (built-in)

Included. Good. Less aggressive warming ramp, better for higher-quality lower-volume campaigns.

Mailwarm

Standalone, large network, dedicated warming. More expensive but often higher quality for advanced use cases.

  • Best for: operators who need warming independent of their sending tool

Warmy

Standalone. Detailed reporting on warming performance. AI-powered engagement patterns.

  • Best for: operators who want visibility into warming quality metrics

MailReach

Standalone. Strong reputation. Used by marketers and agencies.

TrulyInbox

Newer, competitive pricing.

What matters in a warming tool

Network quality

Some warming networks include low-quality mailboxes (themselves damaged from overuse). Sending warming emails to those provides no benefit. Reputable tools curate their networks.

Ramp configuration

Good tools let you configure the daily volume ramp (start low, increase gradually). Default ramps are usually fine for most cases.

Cross-provider coverage

Cross-provider activity may affect results, but provider coverage does not prove a reputation gain. Compare matched mailboxes using single-provider and cross-provider warming. Hold warmup volume, cold sends, audience, copy, and authentication fixed. Review seed placement and each provider's reputation signals after the same fixed window.

My taketest provider coverage

Spam folder recovery

Some tools move their own warming messages from spam to the inbox. Record whether the tool does this, but do not treat that action as proof that campaign mail will reach the inbox.

When warming alone isn't enough

Warming tools can't save:

  • Bad DNS configuration
  • Sending to unverified addresses
  • Spammy content
  • Too-high volume
  • Damaged domain reputation

Warming is an optional variable to test. It does not replace authentication, list hygiene, wanted mail, or complaint monitoring.

A controlled warming trial

  1. Select matched mailboxes with the same provider, authentication, domain age, and starting reputation.
  2. Randomly assign half to warming and half to the control. Set the trial length before starting.
  3. Hold cold-send volume, audience rules, copy, and send windows fixed across both groups.
  4. Compare seed placement, provider reputation, complaints, bounces, and positive replies after the fixed window.
  5. Stop if a guardrail worsens. Continue only if the result is useful, repeatable, and worth the cost.

Do not run warming forever by default. Recheck the decision with a fresh holdout because providers, lists, and sending patterns change.

Instantly and Smartlead describe warmup within plan-specific product limits. Confirm that your selected plan includes the mailboxes and warmup scope you expect.

Where this breaks and how to measure it

Use seed inboxes and provider dashboards to compare a controlled sender ramp with and without the tool. Stop the test if complaints, bounces, or placement worsen.

Do this today

  1. Select matched mailboxes with the same provider, authentication, domain age, and starting reputation. Set the observation window before starting.
  2. Randomly assign half to warming and half to no warming.
  3. Keep cold-send volume, audience rules, copy, and send windows fixed across both groups.
  4. After the fixed window, compare seed placement, provider reputation, complaints, bounces, and positive replies between the groups. Stop if complaints, bounces, or placement worsen. Continue only if warming produces a useful, repeatable improvement worth the cost.
