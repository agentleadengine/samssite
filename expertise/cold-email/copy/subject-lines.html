Subject lines

4 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

The rules

  • Lowercase. Title Case Looks Like Marketing. lowercase feels like a colleague.
  • No emojis. Instantly flags as promotional in B2B inboxes.
  • No symbols or brackets. Avoid reply prefixes, question marks, exclamation marks, and parentheses.
  • No questions unless very natural. "Quick question" is dead.
  • Name-drop when relevant. "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out" works.

Patterns that work

The specific observation

"your post on retention"
"saw your Q3 announcement"
"re: your hiring push"

The question format (short)

"quick thought on pipeline"
"thoughts on [specific topic]"

The name-drop

"[Mutual name] said to reach out"
"from [company] to [their company]"

The curiosity

"odd question about [department]"
"something you might've missed"

Patterns that fail

  • "Exclusive offer for [Company]", marketing
  • "Transform your pipeline", ad
  • "FREE [anything]", spam filter bait
  • "Last chance", fake urgency
  • "Are you struggling with X?", leading
  • "You won't believe this", clickbait

Where this breaks and how to measure it

Pre-register one subject-line contrast and judge it on replies or conversions, not opens alone. Keep sender, audience, offer, and delivery window constant.

Do this today

  1. Write subject lines like a peer emailing a peer, "re: your post on X" beats "boost your ROI with our solution"
  2. Avoid classic spam phrases ("free", "guarantee", "act now"), filters and humans both flag them faster than any content
  3. Reference a specific detail (their role, company, recent event), generic "quick question" feels fake at scale and reply rate shows it
