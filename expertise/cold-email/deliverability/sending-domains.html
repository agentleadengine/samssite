Sending domain strategy

5 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Separate mail streams only after checking your provider's rules and your legal obligations. The goal is to identify each stream, authenticate it, and measure its reputation without relying on a universal domain-capacity formula.

The anti-pattern

"Let's send cold email from sam@yourcompany.com because it has credibility."

Mixing prospecting, employee, and transactional mail makes a delivery problem harder to isolate. Map the streams before choosing domains or subdomains.

The domain architecture

Primary domain

Your brand: yourcompany.com. Used for:

  • Your website
  • Transactional emails (signup, password reset)
  • One-to-one business email (from known addresses, to known addresses)

This domain should never send cold outreach at volume.

Sending domains

Additional domains purchased specifically for cold email: yourcompany.io, getyourcompany.com, try-yourcompany.com, yourcompany-team.com, etc.

Each sending domain:

  • Points to your brand (has a redirect to yourcompany.com, mentions "yourcompany" so recipients trust it)
  • Has its own DNS setup (SPF, DKIM, DMARC)
  • Has a named owner, documented purpose, and separate reporting

Why multiple domains

Microsoft 365 says bulk sending from the service is unsupported and best effort. It recommends separating bulk streams with custom subdomains and authenticating them with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.

Separation makes stream-level reporting possible. It does not guarantee inbox placement or create a fixed daily allowance. Choose the number of streams from real operational needs, then watch provider feedback for each one.

Domain naming

Do

  • Variations of your brand: getyourcompany.com, yourcompany.io, yourcompany-co.com
  • Industry-relevant: yourcompanyhq.com
  • Short, memorable
  • Redirect to your main site when visited

Don't

  • Completely unrelated names that look like spoofing
  • Misspellings of your brand (looks deceptive)
  • Free domains (.tk, .ml etc.), spam-tier from day one

The mailbox-per-domain ratio

  • List each mailbox, owner, provider, campaign, and authenticated domain.
  • Record delivery errors, complaints, replies, and provider reputation by mailbox.
  • Add capacity only after the current group remains stable through a complete campaign cycle.
  • Pause the affected stream when provider feedback worsens. Do not hide the problem by rotating domains.

Redirects and brand trust

When a prospect clicks your signature link or Googles the sending domain, they should land somewhere that looks legit. Minimum setup:

  • A redirect to the main company site, or
  • A minimal page that identifies the company and links to its main site

A bare sending domain with no website is a red flag to sophisticated recipients.

Choose the host from current terms

Check whether the provider supports the intended mail, its current sending limits, authentication setup, export path, admin controls, and abuse policy. Price the exact plan on the day you buy it. A mailbox plan is not evidence that the provider permits a cold-email program.

The launch cost math

Build a quote from current vendor pages. Keep capacity assumptions out of the price sheet until a controlled send produces real delivery data.

  • Domains: quantity multiplied by the registrar's current renewal price, including privacy or transfer fees.
  • Mailboxes: seats multiplied by the host's current plan price and billing term.
  • Sending and data tools: exact plan, credits, overages, and required add-ons.
  • Labor: setup, authentication review, list review, reply handling, and weekly monitoring.
  • Contingency: taxes and renewal changes shown separately, not hidden in a capacity promise.

The subdomain question

Can you use subdomains (outbound.yourcompany.com) instead of separate root domains?

Microsoft recommends custom subdomains to separate bulk streams in Microsoft 365. Other providers can treat identity and reputation differently. Ask each provider how it evaluates the root domain, subdomain, DKIM domain, and sending IP before choosing an architecture.

The checklist for a new sending domain

  1. Register the domain
  2. Set up DNS with the chosen provider
  3. Add SPF, DKIM, DMARC records
  4. Enable DKIM in the email host
  5. Set up redirect to your main site
  6. Create mailboxes
  7. Run a controlled launch with wanted mail and gradual changes
  8. Monitor provider feedback, delivery errors, complaints, and replies

Where this breaks and how to measure it

Price the exact domain, mailbox, sending tool, and data plan on purchase day. Set capacity from observed provider feedback, not a fixed mailbox formula.

Do this today

  1. Inventory employee, transactional, marketing, and prospecting mail streams.
  2. Confirm the provider permits each stream and document its authentication requirements.
  3. Build the launch quote from current domain, mailbox, tool, data, and labor prices.
  4. Launch one controlled stream and increase only from its provider feedback and campaign data.
