Legal rules by jurisdiction

5 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Cold email rules can depend on the sender, recipient, message, data, and jurisdiction. This page is a dated summary, not legal advice. Ask your lawyer about your recipient list, message, and jurisdictions.

United States, CAN-SPAM (2003)

What the federal rule covers

CAN-SPAM covers commercial email, including business-to-business messages. It does not require prior consent for every commercial message. Compliance with CAN-SPAM does not settle other federal, state, sector, contract, or platform rules.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 01

Ask your lawyer which other rules apply to your sender, offer, recipient, and state.

What it requires

  • Accurate "From," "Reply-To," and routing information (no spoofing)
  • Subject lines that reflect email content (no deception)
  • Clear and conspicuous identification that the message is an advertisement (FTC guidance, checked 28 Sep 2026)
  • A valid physical postal address in the email
  • A clear, working unsubscribe mechanism
  • Honoring opt-outs within 10 business days
  • Not transferring the email address after opt-out

Ask your lawyer whether the wording, placement, and formatting in your exact message make that advertising identification clear and conspicuous.

Penalties

The FTC guide states that each separate CAN-SPAM email violation can carry a penalty of up to $53,088. It also says senders must honor opt-outs within 10 business days. Same-day handling is an operational policy, not the legal deadline.

Changing28 Sep 2026Source 01

Canada, CASL (2014)

CASL requires consent, identification information, and an unsubscribe mechanism. Its implied-consent routes are limited and fact-specific, including some published business addresses.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Ask your lawyer whether each Canadian recipient has express or implied consent, what evidence supports it, and when that basis expires.

European Union, GDPR + ePrivacy Directive

EU data-protection rules require a lawful basis to process personal data. Electronic-marketing rules also vary by member state, so a GDPR lawful basis alone does not answer whether a message may be sent.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 04

Ask your lawyer which national electronic-marketing rule applies, whether consent is required, what lawful basis supports data processing, and what notice the recipient must receive.

United Kingdom, PECR + UK GDPR

PECR's electronic-mail marketing rule does not apply to corporate subscribers. Sole traders and some partnerships are treated as individual subscribers and need separate analysis.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 03

Ask your lawyer about the recipient type, the message purpose, and the lawful basis for processing personal data.

Australia, SPAM Act 2003

Australian commercial electronic messages require consent, accurate sender identification, and a working unsubscribe facility.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 05

Ask your lawyer what evidence establishes consent for each recipient and whether the message is covered by the Spam Act.

Questions to take to counsel

No single checklist makes a campaign compliant everywhere. Give counsel a sample message and the actual recipient data, then ask:

  1. Which law applies to each recipient's location and legal status?
  2. Is the message commercial, and does its purpose trigger sector-specific rules?
  3. What consent or other legal basis is required, and what proof must we retain?
  4. Which sender details, notices, and opt-out method must the message contain?
  5. How quickly must suppression occur across every sending system?
  6. Do our data source, retention period, transfers, and vendor contracts meet the applicable rules?

What unsubscribe should look like

The required mechanism depends on the recipient and jurisdiction. These are operational patterns, not universal legal options.

Explicit unsubscribe link

"If you'd prefer I stop reaching out, unsubscribe here: [link]." Test that the link works and suppresses the address in every sending system.

Plain-language opt-out

"If you're not the right person or prefer I don't reach out, just reply and let me know." A reply route can be useful, but it is not a universal substitute for a compliant opt-out mechanism.

Use the opt-out method required for the recipient and jurisdiction. Ask your lawyer whether a reply, link, or both are required for your messages.

The "corporate subscriber" concept

Under UK PECR, corporate-subscriber status depends on the subscriber's legal status. It does not depend on whether the address is role-based or names a person. A named employee at a limited company can still use a corporate-subscriber address. A role address used by a sole trader does not become corporate because of its format. Ask your lawyer how to classify each recipient and how UK GDPR applies to any personal data.

The reputation risk beyond legality

Even legal cold email can hurt your reputation if done carelessly. Legal compliance is the floor, not the ceiling. Respect recipients, stop when told, send less than you could, and prioritize quality of targeting over volume.

Where this breaks and how to measure it

For any recipient list, record jurisdiction, recipient type, message purpose, consent basis, sender identity, and opt-out method. Ask your lawyer to review the unresolved cells.

Do this today

  1. Export one proposed recipient list with country, subscriber type, data source, and consent evidence.
  2. Attach the exact message, sender identity, postal address, privacy notice, and opt-out flow.
  3. Ask counsel which laws apply, whether the message may be sent, and what records you must retain.
  4. Test suppression across every sending tool. Use same-day suppression as your operating policy while preserving each legal deadline.
