What to test

4 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Treat every test priority as a hypothesis, not a promised lift. Randomly assign comparable contacts, change one variable, and compare qualified positive replies after a fixed observation window.

The rankings and numerical ranges shown on this page are planning hypotheses, not measured benchmarks. Test one contrast within one qualified audience. Randomly assign contacts, hold other conditions fixed, and compare qualified positive replies after a preset window.

My takemeasure before adopting

Start with high-decision-value hypotheses

Offer / angle

I would test the offer before polishing small wording choices. One audience may respond differently to "help your AEs ramp faster" and "improve forecast accuracy," but neither angle has a promised lift. Randomly assign qualified contacts, keep the sender and call to action fixed, and compare positive replies after a fixed observation window.

My takemeasure positive replies

Sender identity

Test a truthful founder sender against a truthful sales sender only when either person could handle the reply. Use the same audience, offer, copy, and send window. Compare qualified positive replies, complaints, and bounces before deciding.

Test timing separately

Day of week

Do not assume one weekday or hour works for every B2B audience. Randomly split the same qualified segment across two send windows. Keep the sender and message fixed. Compare positive replies after the same observation period, then repeat the test before changing the schedule.

Save cosmetic contrasts for later

Email signature

Compare a minimal signature with an expanded signature only after the audience and offer are stable. Keep the message and sending conditions fixed, then use the same primary metric and observation window.

Greeting style

If greeting style matters to your brand, test "Hey [name]" against "Hi [name]" with every other word held fixed. Accept an inconclusive result when the reply difference is too small to guide a decision.

Formatting

Plain text is my starting format, not a proven winner for every cold audience. Compare plain text with minimal HTML across randomly assigned contacts. Keep the sender, copy, links, and send window fixed. Check positive replies and complaint rate after the same observation period.

My takerun a controlled comparison

Defer tests that cannot change a decision

  • Font in email (unless you're using something weird)
  • Button color (you probably shouldn't have a button in cold email)
  • Exact greeting punctuation
  • Minor word changes ("sale" → "special offer")

At your current volume, these contrasts may not produce enough replies to guide a decision. Record the hypothesis, expected decision, sample size, and observation window before spending a send on it.

The experiment pipeline

Maintain a running list of hypotheses. Rank them by decision value and implementation cost. Keep a log:

  • Hypothesis tested
  • Variable changed
  • Result (winner / loser / inconclusive)
  • Size of effect
  • What you rolled forward

Rapid iteration without full tests

  1. Read the replies (both positive and negative)
  2. Rewrite based on what you learned
  3. Compare directionally, not statistically

Use these replies to form the next hypothesis. Do not call the rewrite a winner without random allocation and a fixed observation window.

Where this breaks and how to measure it

Rank tests by expected impact and implementation cost. Run one contrast at a time, use a preselected primary metric, and wait for the stated observation window.

Do this today

  1. Write one hypothesis that could change a campaign decision. Name the audience, control, variant, and primary metric.
  2. Randomly assign qualified contacts. Change one variable and hold the sender, offer, and send window fixed.
  3. Set the sample size and observation window before sending. Track positive replies, complaints, and bounces.
  4. Roll forward only when the result supports the hypothesis without worsening a guardrail. Otherwise record it as inconclusive.
