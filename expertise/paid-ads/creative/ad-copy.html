Ad copy for paid

3 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Paid ad copy is short, specific, and action-oriented. It's direct response compressed into 50-150 words. Each element, hook, body, CTA, does a specific job.

The structure

  1. Hook: first line, stops the scroll
  2. Body: 1-3 sentences developing the promise
  3. Proof: a specific number, case study, or credential (optional but strong)
  4. CTA: specific action

Preview each placement

  • Open the current platform preview for every selected placement
  • Check the first visible line on mobile and desktop
  • Capture a screenshot before launch
  • Test a short and a long version with the same creative and audience
  • Judge the test on qualified conversion rate, not clicks alone

Copy rules

Specificity beats generality

Replace a vague promise with a verified price, named feature, concrete action, or authorized customer outcome. The detail must be true for the advertised offer.

One angle per ad

Don't cram 5 benefits. Pick one angle (price, speed, quality, outcome) and commit.

Active voice

"Get X" beats "X can be obtained." Short sentences. Imperative voice for CTAs.

No jargon without translation

Define specialist terms in the language customers use in interviews, search queries, and support conversations.

Read like a text, not a pitch

Especially on Meta. "Just saw this and thought of you" feels personal. "Introducing our revolutionary..." feels corporate.

CTAs that work

  • "Shop now" for commerce
  • "Learn more" for content-led
  • "Get the guide" for lead magnets
  • "Try free" for SaaS
  • "Book call" for services

Emojis

Preview symbols in every selected placement and test them against plain copy:

  • Meta: verify truncation and accessibility text
  • LinkedIn: match the audience's communication style
  • TikTok: confirm the symbol fits the creator's delivery
  • Google: follow the current editorial policy

The P.S. move

If a postscript carries essential qualification or proof, test it against copy that places the same information earlier. Do not assume reading order from the label.

Testing copy

Hold creative, audience, offer, placement, and measurement window constant. Give each version one named angle, such as price, outcome, pain, or story, and size the test before launch.

Do this today

  1. Complete one brief with audience, problem, offer, proof, objection, CTA, placement, and prohibited claim.
  2. Hypothetical brief: Audience: owners of two-location dental offices. Problem: missed calls after 5 p.m. Offer: a 15-minute call-routing review. Proof allowed: a live walkthrough, with no performance claim. Objection: “We already use voicemail.” CTA: “Book the routing review.” Prohibited claims: guaranteed bookings or revenue.
  3. Placement sheet: Prepare a 1:1, 1080 × 1080 image for Feed and a 9:16, 1080 × 1920 version for Stories or Reels. Keep essential text inside the platform preview safe area. Recheck Meta’s placement preview before launch.
  4. Controlled test: A says “How many calls reach voicemail after 5 p.m.?” B says “See where after-hours calls stop.” Randomly assign eligible people to A or B. Hold the image, offer, CTA, placements, budget, and seven-day measurement window constant. Use booked, eligible reviews per assigned eligible person as the primary outcome. If spend is comparable, qualified acquisition cost is another valid primary measure. Keep landing-page conversion rate among visitors as a secondary diagnostic because the copy can change who clicks.
  5. Decision rule: Keep copy that improves qualified outcomes after every selected placement preview passes.
  6. Limitation: Copy results are inseparable from the visual, audience, placement, and offer when those also change.
