Landing pages for paid

3 min readUpdated 2026-04-19

A paid landing page must continue the ad's promise and make the next step clear. Measure the click-to-qualified-outcome path instead of assuming the page caused the loss.

The rules

Match the ad

Headline of landing page matches headline of ad. Promise of ad = promise of page. Mismatch = bounce.

One goal per page

One primary CTA. Not "sign up OR learn more OR read blog." One.

Above-the-fold clarity

Run a brief comprehension check for what the offer is, who it is for, and what happens next.

Mobile-first

Use the campaign's device report. Review the dominant viewport first and verify the other meaningful device segments.

Fast load

Review field Core Web Vitals and lab diagnostics. Segment conversion by device and speed band before claiming business impact.

The structure

  1. Above the fold: headline, subhead, primary CTA, hero image/video
  2. Benefits (not features): 3-5 key benefits as you scroll
  3. Social proof: logos, testimonials, review stars
  4. How it works: brief process or demo
  5. FAQ: objection handling
  6. Second CTA
  7. Footer: basic links

Headline

The most important element. Should:

  • Match ad promise
  • Include core benefit
  • Short enough to scan at the target viewport
  • Readable without hiding the main promise

The form

For lead gen:

  • Ask only what you need
  • Instrument focus, error, abandon, submit, and accepted-lead events
  • Remove one field at a time
  • Compare completion with downstream lead quality
  • Use stages only when they match the task order

The CTA button

  • Specific text ("Get my free plan", not "Submit")
  • High-contrast color
  • Above fold + at bottom
  • Visible without hunting

Social proof

  • Named testimonials with photo (not "John D.")
  • Logos of known clients
  • Specific numbers ("4,000+ customers")
  • Review stars from external platform

Common landing page mistakes

  • Sending traffic to homepage instead of dedicated page
  • Multiple CTAs competing
  • Slow load time
  • Generic hero image (stock photo)
  • No social proof
  • Form with 12 fields
  • Mismatched ad-to-page messaging

Do this today

  1. Put the ad promise, page headline, offer, proof, CTA, and conversion event in one comparison row.
  2. Hypothetical message-match audit: Ad promise: “Map your after-hours call route.” Landing headline: “Book a general marketing consultation.” Mark this as a mismatch. Replace it with “Map your after-hours call route in 15 minutes,” keep the same eligibility note, and repeat the ad CTA above the form.
  3. Speed procedure: Record mobile field data for LCP, INP, and CLS in PageSpeed Insights. Run Lighthouse three times on the same device profile and use the median lab result. Segment landing sessions and completed eligible bookings by device before changing the page.
  4. Decision rule: Fix the first message mismatch or slow element that blocks the named action.
  5. Limitation: A page change may appear to win because the traffic mix changed upstream.
