LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms

3 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Lead Gen Forms open inside LinkedIn and can prefill profile fields. Compare them with a matched landing page because easier completion can also change lead quality.

How they work

  1. User sees ad in feed
  2. Clicks "Download", "Register", "Request"
  3. Form opens with pre-filled fields (user confirms)
  4. User submits
  5. Lead data available via LinkedIn or synced to CRM

Field options

  • First Name, Last Name, Email (pre-filled)
  • Job Title, Company (pre-filled)
  • Phone (not pre-filled)
  • Custom questions

More fields = lower conversion. Keep it short.

LinkedIn says Lead Gen Forms can prefill member profile data. It recommends three to four fields and allows up to twelve.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 01

Best use cases

  • Whitepaper / ebook / report downloads
  • Webinar registrations
  • Demo requests
  • Newsletter subscriptions
  • Trial signups (partial, LinkedIn form captures lead, then you send setup email)

Integration with CRM

LinkedIn integrates natively with HubSpot, Salesforce, Marketo, and others. Set up sync so leads hit your CRM in real time, not via manual download.

Follow-up is critical

Lead Gen Forms generate leads fast but conversion depends on follow-up. Within 5 minutes:

  • Send the promised resource
  • Queue for SDR follow-up
  • Add to nurture sequence

Quality concerns

Lower friction means more curiosity-clickers alongside real intent. Quality varies. Some patterns:

  • Qualify in the form (add a "what's your team size" custom question)
  • Follow up fast and filter by responsiveness
  • Score by role, intern filling out a VP's form is a pattern

Run a controlled comparison

Split a matched audience between a Lead Gen Form and the same offer on a landing page. Keep spend, creative, dates, and follow-up speed equal.

  • Form fill rate: submitted forms divided by clicks
  • Qualified rate: accepted leads divided by submitted forms
  • Lead-to-SQL rate: sales-qualified leads divided by submitted forms
  • Cost per SQL: spend divided by sales-qualified leads

Choose the route with the lower cost per qualified outcome, not the cheaper form fill.

Do this today

  1. Define the minimum fields, consent text, hidden campaign IDs, CRM routing, owner, and response deadline.
  2. Hypothetical form setup: Ask work email, company, role, and employee band. State: “By submitting, you ask ExampleCo to contact you about the payroll review. See the privacy policy.” Send campaign ID, form ID, consent text version, and submission time to the CRM.
  3. Route an eligible lead to the named owner within 15 minutes during business hours. Compare form and landing-page arms by sales-accepted lead rate and opportunity rate after the same follow-up window. Treat the SLA as a planning target, not a claimed result.
  4. Decision rule: Keep the form only if lead-to-SQL cost beats the matched landing page.
  5. Limitation: Prefill reduces typing but may increase accidental or low-intent submissions.
