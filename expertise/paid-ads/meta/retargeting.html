Meta retargeting

4 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Retargeting hits people who've already visited your site, engaged with your content, or started a checkout. Cheaper CAC than prospecting, but easy to overdo.

Retargeting audiences

  • Eligible website visitors: choose a lookback that matches the measured decision cycle
  • Cart abandoners: highest intent, highest conversion
  • Specific product viewers: intent-matched creative
  • Engaged IG/FB users: lower intent but wider reach
  • Email subscribers not purchased: owned data retarget
  • Past customers: set the reactivation window from the observed repurchase cycle

Creative for retargeting

Different creative than prospecting:

  • Reminder of what they viewed
  • Social proof (reviews, testimonials)
  • Offers/discounts (if your margin allows)
  • Urgency (limited stock, deadline)
  • Answers to common objections

Frequency caps

Review frequency alongside reach, qualified-result cost, negative feedback, and conversion lag:

  • Use a shorter lookback when later exposures add cost without incremental results
  • Rotate distinct, approved messages when the deterioration rule is met
  • Exclude people who've already converted

Budget allocation

  • Set prospecting spend from the incremental customer target and allowable acquisition cost
  • Set retargeting spend only after a holdout shows incremental contribution

Retargeting scales with prospecting. More prospecting = bigger retargeting audience. Don't overspend on retargeting when your audience is small.

The attribution gotcha

Retargeting gets credit for conversions that would have happened anyway. True incrementality of retargeting is smaller than reported. Don't overfund it.

Messenger/WhatsApp retargeting

For certain categories (support-heavy, high-consideration purchases), conversations in Messenger or WhatsApp convert higher than web. Test if applicable.

Do this today

  1. Randomly hold out an eligible audience, exclude recent purchasers, and record exposure leakage and CRM revenue.
  2. Hypothetical holdout: Randomly assign eligible site visitors to 90% retargeting and 10% no-retargeting. Exclude purchasers, staff, and users without required consent. Keep the assignment for 30 days.
  3. Compare purchases per assigned eligible user after the same window. Platform-attributed conversions in the exposed group are assisted conversions. The rate difference between exposed and holdout estimates incremental effect, subject to spillover and sample uncertainty.
  4. Decision rule: Fund retargeting from incremental contribution, not platform-attributed orders.
  5. Limitation: Retargeted buyers often had higher purchase intent before the ad.
