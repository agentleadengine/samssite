LTV:CAC

3 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

LTV:CAC compares customer gross profit with acquisition cost. A useful target comes from your margins, retention, refunds, cash timing, and tolerance for payback risk.

Definitions

LTV (Lifetime Value)

Total gross profit a customer generates over their relationship with you.

LTV = Gross profit per customer × years as customer

CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)

Total sales and marketing cost assigned to an acquisition cohort, divided by new customers in that cohort.

CAC = (Media + sales labor + agency + acquisition tools) / New customers

Set a target from the business

The ratios in this illustration are not universal targets. Set a payback rule from observed margin, retention, refunds, and available cash.

  • Start with contribution margin after refunds and fulfillment
  • Use observed retention by acquisition cohort
  • Map when cash arrives and when acquisition spend leaves
  • Choose the longest payback period the business can finance safely

Why a fixed ratio misleads

LTV should use gross profit, not revenue. The remaining margin still needs to cover:

  • Overhead, salaries, infrastructure
  • Product development
  • Non-ad marketing spend
  • Profit/dividends

A low ratio can leave too little contribution to cover overhead. A high ratio can also mean the business is underinvesting. Read the ratio with cohort payback and cash capacity.

Payback period

Payback is how quickly cumulative gross profit recovers CAC. Calculate it by cohort and stop at the first month where cumulative gross profit exceeds acquisition cost. Compare that month with the cash runway available to fund growth.

Improving LTV:CAC

Reduce CAC

  • Better creative → lower CPC, higher conversion
  • Better landing pages
  • Less expensive channels

Increase LTV

  • Upsells and cross-sells
  • Subscription (higher retention)
  • Raise prices
  • Reduce churn
  • Expand offering

The segmentation reveal

Blended LTV:CAC often hides truth. Segment:

  • By channel (Meta CAC vs Google CAC)
  • By customer type
  • By product/offer
  • By cohort

Often some segments are 1.5:1 while others are 8:1. Shifting budget into the 8:1 segments is where growth comes from.

Do this today

Hypothetical cohort: Ten customers start in January. Media costs $2,400 and sales labor costs $600, so fully loaded CAC is $300.

The cohort recovers its $3,000 acquisition cost during month six. A revenue-only view would show faster payback because it ignores delivery cost, refunds, and sales labor.

Decision rule: Spend against observed cohort payback and the cash window the business can finance. This illustration is not a retention forecast.
