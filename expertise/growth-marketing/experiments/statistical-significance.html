Statistical significance

3 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Key concepts

A p-value is conditional. It is the probability, under the null hypothesis and the test's model assumptions, of seeing a result at least this extreme. It is not the probability that the result is random or that the null is true.

  • Alpha: the false-positive threshold chosen before the test.
  • Power: the chance of detecting the planned effect when it is real.
  • Effect size: the difference the decision needs to detect.
  • Confidence interval: a range from the estimate and sampling model, not a list of guaranteed true values.

Common mistakes

  • Stopping when the p-value first crosses a threshold.
  • Testing many variants without adjusting the analysis.
  • Treating a non-significant result as proof of no effect.

The threshold belongs to the decision

Choose alpha and power from false-positive cost, missed-effect cost, feasible traffic, and the action the result will trigger. Do not inherit a threshold without that reasoning.

Choose alpha and power for the decision

NIST describes alpha as a chosen significance level. An alpha of 0.05 is common, not universal.

A worked two-proportion test

Illustrative assumptions, not observed results: a quote form converts 4.0% in the control. A 1.5 percentage-point increase is worth acting on. Choose a two-sided alpha of 0.05 and power of 0.80. A standard normal approximation gives about 3,152 eligible visitors per group. The arithmetic uses the 4.0% baseline, 5.5% target, 1.96 critical value, and 0.84 power value.

Now suppose an illustrative readout has 80 qualified quotes from 2,000 control visitors and 110 from 2,000 challenger visitors. The rates are 4.0% and 5.5%. The difference is 1.5 percentage points. The pooled standard error is 0.00673, so z = 0.015 / 0.00673 = 2.23. The two-sided p-value is about 0.026. An unpooled 95% confidence interval for the difference is about 0.18 to 2.82 percentage points.

The p-value says how surprising this or a more extreme difference would be under the null and the model assumptions. It does not show a 97.4% chance that the challenger is better. The illustrative test also stopped below its planned 3,152 visitors per group, so the team must not pretend it followed the registered power plan.

Where it breaks: The calculation assumes independent, correctly classified visitors and stable assignment. Repeated peeking, missing events, bot traffic, or changed eligibility can invalidate the readout.

Do this today

  1. Export the baseline numerator and denominator.
  2. Write the smallest effect that changes a decision.
  3. Choose alpha, power, and allocation before launch.
  4. Save the formula, stopping rule, interval, and decision together.
