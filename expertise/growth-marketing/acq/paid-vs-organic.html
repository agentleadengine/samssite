Paid vs organic

3 min readUpdated 2026-04-19

Paid and organic acquisition have opposite profiles. Paid produces predictable volume at ongoing cost. Organic (SEO, content, word-of-mouth) builds slower but compounds.

Paid characteristics

  • Immediate traffic
  • Ongoing cost
  • Control over volume
  • Stops when budget stops

Organic characteristics

  • Learning arrives after publishing, indexing, distribution, and the offer cycle
  • Compounds
  • Lower per-user cost over time
  • Hard to control volume

When paid wins

Validated offer, need fast test or scale, unit economics support it.

When organic wins

Long-term moat building, compounds value, differentiated content or brand.

The pragmatic answer

Run paid to validate and scale what works. Build organic in parallel for long-term defensibility.

Worked paid vs organic record

Illustrative assumptions, not observed results: Both channels promote the same bookkeeping offer for 90 days and count a new paying customer after a qualified call. Paid costs $6,000 in media plus $1,000 in landing-page work and produces 24 customers. Organic costs $7,000 in research, writing, and distribution and produces 16 customers. CAC is $7,000 / 24 = $291.67 for paid and $7,000 / 16 = $437.50 for organic. At $200 monthly contribution per customer, estimated cash payback is 1.46 months versus 2.19 months.

Where it breaks: The 90-day comparison ignores organic traffic after the window and paid conversions that arrive late. Shared branded demand can also credit both channels for the same customer.

Do this today

  1. Fix one offer and customer event.
  2. Load labor and media cost for the same window.
  3. Calculate CAC and contribution payback.
  4. Record what happens outside the window separately.
