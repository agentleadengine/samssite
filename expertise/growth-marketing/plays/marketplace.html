Marketplace growth

3 min readUpdated 2026-04-19

Marketplaces need supply AND demand. Growth = solving cold start then building liquidity, then scale.

Cold start

  • Pick a narrow niche
  • Fake one side if needed (manual concierge, curated inventory)
  • Grow one side first

Liquidity metrics

  • Match rate
  • Time to match
  • Supply/demand balance

Network effects

Each new user makes the product more valuable for others, the moat.

Worked marketplace record

Choose one city and one service category. Liquidity means a buyer request receives an acceptable supplier match before the promised deadline. Track eligible requests, qualified suppliers, match time, acceptance, and completion. Add supply or demand only where the failed transition occurs.

Illustrative city-week: 200 eligible home-cleaning requests, 150 matched within 30 minutes, 120 accepted, and 105 completed. Liquidity is 150 / 200 = 75%; the largest loss is the 50 unmatched requests. If failure clusters in two postal codes during evenings, recruit evening supply there before buying more citywide demand.

Where it breaks: A citywide average hides local shortages, and fast matches can be low quality. Cancellations and repeat booking must guard the liquidity measure.

Do this today

  1. Choose city, category, and match deadline.
  2. Calculate match, acceptance, and completion by zone and hour.
  3. Locate the largest failed transition.
  4. Assign a supply or demand intervention to that cell.
