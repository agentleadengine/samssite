A/B testing setup

3 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

A/B testing splits traffic between control (A) and variant (B), measures outcomes, picks winner.

Prerequisites

  • Clear hypothesis
  • Single variable changed
  • Planned sample size calculated
  • Primary metric and guardrails defined

Setup

  • Predeclared allocation
  • Random, persistent assignment
  • Both variants tracked identically
  • Fixed-sample or valid sequential stopping rule chosen before launch

Post-launch

For a fixed-sample test, analyze after the planned sample and duration are complete. Statistical significance alone is not a stopping rule or a winner declaration. Check the effect interval, guardrails, and sample-ratio mismatch.

The checklist uses a fixed-sample plan. “No peeking” means do not change the decision because an interim fixed-horizon p-value looks favorable.

Hypothetical preregistered protocol:

FieldFrozen choice
HypothesisShowing delivery timing beside the CTA changes completed checkout rate
PopulationNew desktop and mobile visitors eligible to buy, excluding staff and bots
Allocation50% control, 50% variant, persistent by user ID
Primary metricPurchasers divided by eligible assigned users
Planning inputs8.0% baseline checkout rate, 8.8% minimum useful variant rate, two-sided 5% alpha, and 80% power
Calculated sample18,872 eligible assigned users per arm, 37,744 total, using the normal approximation for two independent proportions
Duration13 full weeks at 3,000 eligible users per week. Do not extend past 15 weeks without closing and planning a new test.
GuardrailsRefund rate no higher than 5.0%, support contacts no higher than 0.35 per order, and page-error rate no higher than 0.5%
StoppingAnalyze once after both arms reach 18,872 users and 13 full weeks have elapsed. Stop early only for broken assignment, tracking failure, or a guardrail breach.

The thresholds are hypothetical planning inputs, not observed results. Before reading the effect, check assignment, exposure logging, sample-ratio mismatch, missing values, and guardrails. Investigate a mismatch instead of adding an arbitrary buffer.

Decision rule: Decide at the planned sample from the effect interval and every predeclared guardrail.
