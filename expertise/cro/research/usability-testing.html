Usability testing

3 min readUpdated 2026-04-19

Usability testing has users attempt tasks on your site, narrating thoughts. Uncovers confusion, friction, mental-model mismatches.

Method

Give each participant a realistic task without teaching the route. Ask them to think aloud, then record completion, errors, recoveries, and comments.

Sample size

NN/g recommends about five participants for many small qualitative studies, with more needed for distinct user groups and quantitative measurement.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 01

Iteration

Fix repeated blockers, then run a fresh round with representative participants. Do not use a small qualitative round to estimate population frequency.

Do this today

  1. Recruit from one named user group and give each person the same realistic task without teaching the path.
  2. Hypothetical study: Recruit people who bought business insurance in the last year and use the device mix seen in analytics. Task: “Find coverage for a new work van and tell me when you would stop.” Prompts: “What are you looking for?” and “What did you expect?” Do not teach the interface.
  3. Run five sessions, fix severe repeated blockers, then recruit a fresh batch. This finds usability problems. It does not estimate their population frequency or prove a conversion lift.
  4. Decision rule: Fix repeated blockers, rerun the task, and recruit separately when another user group matters.
  5. Limitation: A small qualitative study reveals failure modes. It does not measure their population rate.
