Expertise / RAG

Compare methods on one query set, one metric, and one latency budget.

RAG can retrieve wrong or irrelevant context. Compare every method on held-out examples before changing production retrieval.

Use one benchmark sheet

Start with 200 held-out queries. Label every relevant document for each query. Report recall@10, mean reciprocal rank at 10, 95th percentile latency, and cost per 1,000 queries. Split results for names, product codes, short questions, and vague questions.

MethodWhat changesCost and latency assumptionFailure to inspect
BM25Exact and weighted termsOne sparse searchSynonyms and paraphrases
DenseEmbedding similarityOne query embedding plus vector searchNames, codes, and near-duplicate meaning

HyDEEmbed a hypothetical answerOne model call before searchHypothesis steers retrieval toward a wrong topic
RerankingReorder a larger candidate setOne reranker call after retrievalBetter rank metric but slower answers

A published baseline, with limits

BEIR reports nDCG@10 across named retrieval datasets. On TREC-COVID, BM25 scored 0.656 and BM25 plus a cross-encoder scored 0.757. On ArguAna, the same two methods scored 0.315 and 0.311. One reranker helped the first dataset and slightly hurt the second. That is why an average cannot choose your production method.

In BEIR's one-million-document DBPedia test, BM25 averaged 20 ms per query on the stated CPU setup. BM25 plus a cross-encoder averaged 450 ms on the stated GPU setup. These are paper results on its hardware, not a latency promise for your stack.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Your result is unknown until you test your corpus. Use the 200-query sheet above, keep the same candidates and relevance labels, and record nDCG@10 or recall@10 beside 95th percentile latency. Report each query slice separately.

Read the result by query slice

A single average hides the decision. BM25 may rescue product codes. Dense retrieval may rescue paraphrases. Rewriting may help vague questions while damaging exact names. Keep the method only if its target slice improves without breaking access filters or the latency budget.

Do this today

  1. Label 200 queries and their relevant documents.
  2. Run BM25 and dense baselines first.
  3. Add one method and record recall@10, MRR@10, 95th percentile latency, and cost.
  4. Keep it only for the query slice it improves.
