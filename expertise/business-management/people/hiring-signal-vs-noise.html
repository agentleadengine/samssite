Hiring, signal vs noise

5 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

What predicts job performance (validated research)

Use evidence that resembles the work: a structured work sample, consistent questions, and a role scorecard. Validate the process against later job outcomes instead of treating any method as universally predictive.

The core principle: predict work by watching work

A candidate should complete a bounded, compensated exercise that samples a real role outcome without producing free production work.

Stage 2: Sourcing

Apply the same eligibility criteria to every source and record source quality through accepted offers and later performance.

Stage 4: Hiring manager interview

Ask the same outcome-linked questions, score evidence independently, and record follow-up questions before discussion with other interviewers.

Stage 6: Panel interviews

Assign each interviewer a distinct competency and prohibit repeated conversational interviews that add no new evidence.

Stage 7: References

With the candidate's permission, verify role, scope, working relationship, and specific examples relevant to the scorecard.

Stage 8: Offer

Map the offer to the documented level and compensation range, then state responsibilities, contingencies, and decision deadline plainly.

The "no hire" is cheaper

Compare vacancy cost with the modeled cost of poor fit, replacement, and delayed outcomes. Do not use a universal salary multiplier.

Interview questions that actually reveal something

  • Credits team members for wins
  • Asks substantive questions about the role + business
  • Articulates tradeoffs + uncertainty
  • Can describe their impact in numbers
  • References check out with specifics

The most underrated signal

How do they treat the receptionist, coordinator, and anyone not in the interview? The real personality shows up when nobody's "watching."

Predict the work by sampling the work

Turn the role scorecard into a short work sample with the same instructions, time box, rubric, and reviewer process for every candidate. Record evidence for each score.

Define the outputs the role must produce and evidence that predicts them. Calculate a poor hire from recruiting, pay, ramp, replacement, manager time, and missed work.

If a hire fails, calculate recruiting cost, paid ramp, manager time, replacement cost, and delayed output from company records. Do not use a salary multiplier.

Where it breaks: A work sample can favor prior access to the same tools. Keep it job-related, accessible, and short enough not to become unpaid client work.

Do this today

  1. Name one first-quarter role outcome.
  2. Create a small work sample for that outcome.
  3. Write the scoring rubric before interviews.
  4. Record interviewer evidence independently.
