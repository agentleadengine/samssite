Firing well

6 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Why firing is worth doing well

  • Team performance. Tolerating low performance pulls the team down. Your A-players notice. They leave.
  • Culture. Culture is what you tolerate. Tolerating the wrong behaviors teaches them.
  • The person. Keeping someone in a role they're failing at is cruel, not kind. A clean exit is often a relief + a career open.
  • Legal exposure. Undocumented performance + surprise terminations = lawsuits. Documented + fair = safer.

Types of termination

1. Performance-based

Someone isn't hitting their scorecard. Performance improvement plan (PIP) either turns it around or leads to separation.

2. Conduct-based

Someone violated policy, ethics, or safety. Often immediate termination for severe cases; progressive discipline for minor.

3. Layoff / restructuring

No performance issue, the role no longer exists or the business needs change. Fundamentally different from the above.

4. Role mismatch / mutual

Neither pure performance nor pure role change, the person was a B+ at the role but a D at the role the role became. Often resolved by moving the person to a different role or amicable exit.

The progressive framework (performance cases)

Stage 1: Direct feedback

Name the expected outcome, the observed gap, and the evidence. Give the employee a chance to correct factual errors. Save the date and follow-up.

Stage 2: Formal performance improvement plan (PIP)

Use measurable job outcomes, available support, review dates, and possible consequences. Ask an employment lawyer or HR professional whether this step fits the situation and location.

Stage 3: Decision point

  • If the documented outcomes are met, close the plan and keep normal feedback going.
  • If they are not met, review the record and alternatives with the authorized decision-maker.
  • Before any termination, confirm final pay, benefits, access removal, property return, and communication.

The purpose of the record is a fair, explainable decision. It is not a script for every employment situation.

Set severance from the actual obligations

The federal Fair Labor Standards Act does not require severance pay. An agreement, benefit plan, or applicable state law may still create an obligation.

Do not start from a universal weeks-per-year formula. Review the agreement, written plan, location, final-pay rules, benefits, and release language. Ask an employment lawyer what the company may offer and what the release must contain.

I separate the performance record from the exit checklist. That makes it easier to see whether the decision and the logistics are both complete.

My takemanager workflow

Where it breaks: Performance notes do not replace an investigation, accommodation process, protected-leave review, or contract analysis. Ask an employment lawyer which steps apply before acting.

Do this today

  1. Gather the signed agreement and plan documents.
  2. Confirm final-pay and benefits steps.
  3. Have counsel review the offer and release.
  4. Prepare a short logistics sheet.
