Data + IP protection

7 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Your data and IP are probably your most valuable assets and, in most small companies, your most poorly protected ones. A few hours of deliberate work at the right moments prevents years of pain later. The things to get right aren't complicated. They're just easy to defer until after something bad happens.

IP, what you actually own

In a normal company, your IP includes:

  • Source code
  • Product designs, specs, research
  • Customer lists, sales playbooks, pricing data
  • Trademarks, brand
  • Trade secrets (internal processes, proprietary methods)
  • Data you've collected

Whether you actually own all of it depends on whether the paper trail says so.

The foundational IP documents

1. IP assignment agreements

For employees, the signed agreement should identify the work covered, the rights assigned, confidentiality duties, and what happens to company materials at exit. Do not assume a job title transfers every right.

2. Contractor agreements (not handshakes)

Before a contractor starts, put ownership, pre-existing tools, third-party materials, repository access, and handoff duties in writing. Ask an IP lawyer to review the language for the work and jurisdiction.

Trademark and data controls

  • Keep a dated list of brand names, logos, domains, and the markets where each is used.
  • Classify customer, employee, financial, and product data by sensitivity.
  • Grant access by role and remove it during offboarding.
  • Record backup tests, restore results, and incident-response exercises.

Related: Risk management basics · Business insurance · Vendor management

Use a jurisdiction-specific incident procedure

Under GDPR Article 33, a controller reports a qualifying personal-data breach without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after becoming aware of it. The exception is a breach unlikely to risk people's rights and freedoms.

California breach notice timing depends on the current statute and the facts of the incident. A one-line universal clock is not a safe operating rule.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Write a response card for every jurisdiction where you hold personal information. Name the owner, counsel, regulator link, evidence log, and notice approver. Ask a privacy lawyer which rules apply to your data and incident.

Where it breaks: An asset list does not prove ownership, and a breach checklist does not decide which law applies. Missing signatures or unclear data locations require legal review.

Do this today

  1. List the jurisdictions tied to your data.
  2. Assign an incident owner and backup.
  3. Add counsel and regulator links.
  4. Run one tabletop breach.
