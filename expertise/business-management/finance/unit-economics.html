Unit economics

8 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

If you can't explain how one more customer makes you more profitable or less, you don't have a business model, you have a pile of transactions. Unit economics is the discipline of reducing the entire business down to what happens with one additional customer, one additional job, one additional unit sold. Everything else, fundraising, hiring, strategy, is downstream of this.

The core equation

Contribution per customer equals collected revenue minus the variable costs required to serve that customer. Cash payback divides acquisition cash by the contribution cash received over time.

For a services business

Include delivery labor, contractor cost, transaction fees, and rework at the engagement level. Separate unused capacity from variable delivery cost so staffing choices remain visible.

CAC, the trap

  • Payback period is tracked monthly
  • Your CAC calculation includes all fully-loaded costs
  • When the marketing team pitches a new channel, you model it in unit economics terms before approving spend

Related: Pricing frameworks · Funnel math · Churn diagnostics

Calculate from one acquisition cohort

Use customers acquired in the same period and segment. Acquisition cost is cohort sales and marketing cost divided by new customers. Cash payback is the time until cumulative contribution margin covers that acquisition cost.

Treat lifetime from churn as a simplifying model, not a forecast. Calculate cash payback from acquisition cash outflow, contribution cash inflow, and the timing of both for each cohort.

The shortcut 1 divided by monthly churn describes an idealized constant-churn lifetime. Label it as a simplifying assumption. Cohort retention and margin are better for an operating decision.

Where it breaks: Blended acquisition cost can hide large differences by channel, contract size, sales motion, and cohort age.

Do this today

  1. Choose one acquisition month and segment.
  2. Sum its acquisition cost and new customers.
  3. Track monthly contribution margin until payback.
  4. Write the cash decision the curve supports.
