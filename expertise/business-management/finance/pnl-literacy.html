P&L literacy for operators

7 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

You don't need an accounting degree to read a P&L, but you need to read it well enough to catch when the story the numbers tell contradicts the story your team is telling you. The P&L is the single most important document in the business. Most operators glance at it. A few actually understand it.

The structure

Operating margin (EBITDA %)

Use the ledger's operating income and the company's stated accounting policy. Reconcile excluded items before comparing periods or businesses.

P&L vs cash

The P&L records revenue and expense under an accounting basis; cash follows collection and payment timing. A profitable period can still consume cash when receivables grow or obligations come due.

Build a margin bridge from the ledger

Start with revenue, subtract direct delivery costs for gross profit, then subtract operating expenses for operating profit. State the accounting basis and period. Do not compare the result to an unnamed industry range.

Reconcile revenue, direct costs, operating costs, and accounting basis. Build a margin bridge between periods, then divide each customer’s revenue by total revenue for the same period.

Customer concentration is one customer's revenue divided by total revenue for the same period. Show the numerator and denominator, then decide what exposure the business can accept.

Where it breaks: Account mapping, revenue recognition, owner compensation, and one-time items can move margins without changing operations.

Do this today

  1. Export one closed month's ledger.
  2. Map every account to revenue, direct cost, or operating expense.
  3. Calculate gross margin and top-customer concentration.
  4. Explain the largest change from the prior month.
