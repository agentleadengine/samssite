Meetings that don't waste time

4 min readLast checked 2026-09-28
  1. Is this a decision that requires real-time debate? (Meeting yes.)
  2. Does this need creative collaboration, whiteboard, brainstorm? (Meeting yes.)
  3. Does this need high-bandwidth social connection, relationship, culture? (Meeting yes.)
  4. Is this information that could be an email, memo, or doc? (Meeting NO.)
  5. Is this status updates? (Async in writing.)

Default: no. Make people justify "why a meeting."

Meeting types + their formats

Decision meetings

Send the decision, owner, alternatives, evidence, and deadline before the call. Use the meeting for disputed assumptions. End with the decision, dissent, owner, and due date in writing.

Standups

Use a standup only when today's work has dependencies that need live coordination. Status belongs in the shared board. The meeting should resolve blockers or assign them.

All-hands

Use an all-hands for context employees need at the same time, plus questions leadership will answer. Publish the record afterward for absent people and future hires.

Rules that save hours

Defaults

  • Invite: names the outcome, owner, preparation, and decision deadline.
  • Attendance: includes decision-makers and essential contributors, not spectators.
  • Opening: restates the decision and unresolved facts.
  • Close: records decisions, actions, owners, and dates.
  • Brainstorming: begin with silent written ideas so the first speaker does not set the whole frame.

The annual meeting audit

Do not wait for an annual cleanup. Review one recurring-meeting cycle whenever the decision, team, or workflow changes.

Measure decision latency and attendance cost

For each recurring meeting, record attendees, duration, loaded hourly cost, intended decision, actual decision, and time from issue opened to decision. Change one format rule for one cycle, then compare the same fields.

Illustrative calculation: six people at an assumed loaded cost of $60 an hour in a 45-minute meeting cost 6 x $60 x 0.75 = $270, before preparation. The number is an example. Replace it with payroll or contractor cost before using it.

Where it breaks: A cheaper meeting can create slower decisions or more rework. Cost, decision latency, and action completion must stay together.

Do this today

  1. Choose one recurring meeting.
  2. Write its decision, attendees, duration, and loaded cost.
  3. Record the decision and action owners at close.
  4. Compare latency and completion after one format change.
