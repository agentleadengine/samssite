Funnel math that matters

7 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

The stages

  1. Suspects, addressable market you've reached in some way (impressions, reached leads)
  2. Leads, expressed some interest (opened email, visited site, filled form)
  3. Qualified, matches ICP, has real need, decision authority confirmed
  4. Opportunity, specific deal in discussion, budget confirmed
  5. Proposal, offer presented
  6. Closed-Won, signed

The rates that matter

Use step conversion, elapsed time, and absolute loss for a defined cohort. Keep the stage definitions and eligibility rule beside every rate.

Where to look for the bottleneck

  • Calculate step conversion from the same eligible cohort and observation window.
  • Compare both the loss rate and the absolute number lost at each transition.
  • Check whether stage definitions or delayed updates explain an apparent drop.
  • Prioritize the step where a feasible improvement produces the most additional wins.

Related: Pipeline design · Unit economics · Churn diagnostics

Set coverage from observed conversion

Required qualified opportunities equal the target wins divided by the historical win rate for the same segment and stage definition. Add timing by using the observed sales-cycle distribution, not a fixed coverage multiple.

Keep arithmetic as a labeled example. Set required pipeline from target, observed win rate, cycle time, seller capacity, and the confidence used for the plan.

Illustrative example: if the target is 8 wins and the matching cohort won 2 of every 10 qualified opportunities, the point estimate is 8 / 0.20 = 40 opportunities. Replace both inputs with company data and show uncertainty.

Where it breaks: Small cohorts, changing stage definitions, and open deals that have not matured can make coverage look safer than it is.

Do this today

  1. Freeze one segment and close-date cohort.
  2. Calculate stage conversion and cycle time.
  3. Translate the target into required opportunities.
  4. Compare that need with rep capacity.
