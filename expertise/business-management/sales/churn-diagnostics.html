Churn diagnostics

7 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

You can grow through the front door as fast as you want, if the back door is leaking, you're filling a bathtub without a plug. Churn is the second-most-important number in any recurring-revenue business after new bookings. And most teams don't diagnose it well enough to fix it.

The two churn numbers

  • Logo churn: customers lost divided by customers at the start of the period.
  • Revenue churn: recurring revenue lost divided by starting recurring revenue.

Gross vs, net

  • Gross revenue retention excludes expansion and shows how much starting revenue remained.
  • Net revenue retention includes expansion from the starting customer cohort.

Voluntary vs, involuntary churn

  • Voluntary churn follows a customer decision, such as poor fit, low adoption, or a competing priority.
  • Involuntary churn follows a failed payment or administrative lapse.
  • Keep the two causes separate because the remedies differ.

What good looks like

  • Health scores trigger proactive outreach before renewal conversations
  • Churn root causes feed product roadmap, onboarding fixes, and ICP refinement

Related: Customer success ops · Unit economics · Funnel math

Calculate churn from a defined cohort

Gross revenue retention is starting recurring revenue minus churn and contraction, divided by starting recurring revenue. Net revenue retention adds expansion to that numerator. Use the same customers, currency, period, and exclusions.

Define cohort, start date, segment, gross-retention numerator and denominator, and net-retention treatment. Calculate involuntary loss and recovery from that same cohort without a universal grade.

Tag involuntary churn from the billing failure and recovery record. Calculate its share from the company's cohort instead of using a generic recovery rate.

Where it breaks: Reactivations, upgrades, currency changes, and partial periods can move retention without reflecting product behavior.

Do this today

  1. Freeze one starting customer cohort.
  2. Separate churn, contraction, expansion, and billing failure.
  3. Calculate gross and net retention.
  4. Choose one root cause for review.
