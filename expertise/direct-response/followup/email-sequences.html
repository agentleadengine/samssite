Email sequences

7 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

The sequence types

1. Welcome / indoctrination

Deliver what the person requested, identify the sender, set the expected cadence, and give a working opt-out. A later sales message should follow the consent and purpose recorded at signup.

Segmentation

Segment by a declared event and window, not labels such as hot or cold:

  • Requested resource: delivered, consumed, or not consumed.
  • Product behavior: trial started, key action completed, or stalled.
  • Commercial state: no offer, offer viewed, purchased, refunded, or suppressed.

Define how each event is collected and when the subscriber exits the sequence.

Deliverability basics

  • Authenticate the sending domain and keep its DNS records current.
  • Use the same suppression list across broadcasts and automations.
  • Test the opt-out and record the time from request to suppression.
  • Monitor delivery, complaints, opt-outs, and qualified outcomes by cohort.

Choose the tool from the workflow

Write the entry event, message schedule, data fields, suppression behavior, approval role, and export needs first. Then test those requirements in the current vendor documentation. A familiar editor is not enough if suppression or event history is unreliable.

Measure follow-up by recipient cohort

US commercial email must include a working opt-out, and opt-out requests must be honored within 10 business days. The sender remains responsible when a vendor sends for it.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 01

Gmail's sender rules cover authentication, DNS, TLS, formatting, and spam-rate monitoring. Some senders have added volume-based requirements.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Give each sequence a consent source, purpose, entry, exit, suppression rule, and owner. Report net revenue, opt-outs, and complaints per delivered recipient by segment.

Where it breaks: Opens can be unreliable, revenue windows can overlap, and one person can enter through several addresses. Use delivered recipients, suppression records, and a stated attribution window.

Do this today

  1. Trace one subscriber through suppression.
  2. Test the opt-out.
  3. Define the revenue window.
  4. Review complaints by segment.

The sequence record

MessagePurposeExit condition
Requested assetDeliver the promised item and identify senderDelivery failure or opt-out
Use caseHelp the recipient complete one relevant actionAction completed
OfferInvite an eligible recipient to the next stepReply, purchase, opt-out, or sequence end

Save consent source, entry event, message purpose, send time, delivered state, opt-out, complaint, reply, qualified outcome, revenue, attribution window, and suppression reason. Illustrative cohort: 9,500 delivered recipients, $28,500 net attributed revenue, 190 opt-outs, and 19 complaints. Net revenue per delivered recipient is $3.00; opt-out rate is 2.0% and complaint rate is 0.2%. These are example calculations, not observed results.
