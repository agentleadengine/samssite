AIDA, PAS, PASTOR

7 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

AIDA. Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

  1. Attention: earn the next sentence with a relevant, supportable opening.
  2. Interest: explain the problem or mechanism in the reader's context.
  3. Desire: connect evidence and benefits to the reader's decision.
  4. Action: state the next step and what happens after it.
  5. VSLs (video sales letters)
  6. High-ticket offers where the reader needs the full story before buying
  7. Coaching, courses, services, categories where trust and transformation matter

Why the story matters

A story can supply sequence, context, and stakes, but it must connect to the supported mechanism and decision rather than stand in for proof.

The formulas don't replace thinking

A formula-filled piece of copy with a weak offer or a wrong market still fails. The formula organizes persuasion; it doesn't create persuasion out of nothing. If you're hitting a wall on the copy, the problem is usually upstream, offer, market, or dream customer, not the formula.

Use formulas as checklists

Keep AIDA, PAS, and PASTOR as writing structures. Label every number as a worked example, then measure qualified response and revenue with the same audience and offer.

Where it breaks: A formula can organize weak evidence just as neatly as strong evidence.

Do this today

  1. Choose one formula for one page.
  2. Label each section's job.
  3. Remove unsupported examples.
  4. Register one sequence comparison.
