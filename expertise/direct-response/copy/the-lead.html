The lead (first 100 words)

6 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

What the lead must do

  1. Deliver on the headline. If the headline promised a benefit, the lead starts delivering it.
  2. Build connection. The reader recognizes themselves in the first lines.
  3. Create momentum. Each sentence pulls into the next. Short, clear, declarative.
  4. Establish trust. The lead is where skepticism peaks. A specific claim, a specific detail, or a concrete story lowers it.

The six lead types

2. Promise lead

State a relevant, supportable benefit early and make the conditions visible before the reader can mistake it for a guarantee.

3. Problem-solution lead

Describe the problem in the reader's terms, then introduce the solution category without overstating urgency or certainty.

5. Secret / curiosity lead

Open a specific information gap that the next section actually closes. Do not hide the topic or manufacture mystery around an ordinary fact.

6. Proclamation / news lead

Use this form only for a verifiable change, release, finding, or deadline, with the date and relevance stated plainly.

The "you" lead

  • Begin with a situation the intended reader recognizes.
  • Use second person only when the statement is specific and supportable.

Lead structure, the 100-word budget

  • Sentence 1 earns attention without hiding the subject.
  • The next lines establish the problem, stakes, or mechanism.
  • The final line opens a clear reason to continue.

Lead patterns that work in 2026

Specificity and sensory detail

Choose details that clarify the setting, task, or consequence. Decorative detail slows the lead when it does not change understanding.

The admission

A relevant limitation can establish scope and trust, but it should not become a scripted confession that distracts from the offer's real constraints.

The direct question

Ask a question the reader must answer in their head. "When's the last time you measured your customer acquisition cost, not blended, but by channel?" Engages the reader's mind in a way a statement doesn't.

What to avoid in the lead

  • Do not delay the topic with autobiography or scene-setting that serves no decision.
  • Do not imply outcomes the evidence cannot support.
  • Use "you" before "I" or "we."
  • Add one specific detail, a number, a moment, a scene.
  • Read aloud. If you'd skim past it as a reader, rewrite again.

Treat the opening as a hypothesis

Treat lead length and writing time as heuristics. Compare versions using completion, qualified response, and revenue for the same audience, offer, and window.

Where it breaks: Scroll depth can rise because readers are confused, not persuaded.

Do this today

  1. Name the reader and problem in one sentence.
  2. State the supported promise.
  3. Remove setup that delays the decision.
  4. Register one opening test.
