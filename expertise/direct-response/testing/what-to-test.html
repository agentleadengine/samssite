What to test

5 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Choose a test for the decision it can inform, not a promised lift. Evidence, sample needs, implementation cost, and downside belong in the same queue.

Start with a decision-sized change

Price

  • Fix the product, terms, audience, and refund policy.
  • Use revenue or contribution per eligible visitor as the outcome.
  • Guard conversion quality, refunds, support load, and retention.
  • Compute the minimum price effect the available traffic can detect.

A small detectable effect may still matter for a cheap change. A large idea may be untestable if the traffic or downside is insufficient.

Test one variable, not three

The temptation: "let's test a new headline, new image, and new button all at once and compare to the old." Result: you learn nothing about which element actually drove the change.

Test one thing at a time. If you've already found a winning combination through isolated tests, then test the stacked combination against the old stack, but even then, understand you're testing systems, not variables.

Sequential vs, concurrent

Concurrent tests can interact when they share people, pages, inventory, or downstream outcomes. Map those overlaps before launch. Run unrelated tests together only when assignment and analysis keep them separate.

The "we already know what works" trap

A control stays useful only while its audience, offer, channel, and measurement remain comparable. Recheck it when one of those conditions changes.

Multi-armed bandit vs. A/B

Advanced: multi-armed bandit algorithms dynamically shift traffic toward winners during the test. Good for long-running optimization where you value ongoing performance over clean A/B comparisons. Most teams are better off with straight A/B until they've exhausted obvious tests.

Rank tests by decision value

Sample size depends on baseline, detectable effect, alpha, power, and design. Expected lift cannot come from a universal tier.

Objective advertising claims need a reasonable basis before publication. Evidence and claim risk belong in test selection.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Score the decision informed, evidence, implementation cost, downside risk, and whether traffic supports the precomputed effect. Keep the inputs beside the score.

Where it breaks: A high score built from guesses is still a guess. Re-score when traffic, cost, evidence, or claim risk changes.

Do this today

  1. Write each decision.
  2. Attach evidence and cost.
  3. Compute detectable effect.
  4. Pick a credible test.
