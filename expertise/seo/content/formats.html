Content formats

8 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Content format is the shape your content takes. A guide. A listicle. A comparison. Match the format to the intent of the query, and your ranking chances go up dramatically. Mismatch, and no amount of quality saves you. This page walks through the major formats, how to identify which one the SERP expects, and how to avoid the "format fatigue" that makes a content library feel generic.

The mindset

Format is intent in a physical shape. A user searching "best CRM for insurance" has already decided what kind of answer would help them. Google has already confirmed it by ranking listicles. Don't fight them, feed them what they want.

Three query-shaped examples

I checked these result sets on 28 Sep 2026. “Best CRM for insurance agents” favors ranked comparisons. “How to automate customer follow-up with AI” favors step-by-step guides. “What is an AI agent” favors concise explainers from known technology sources.

Worked brief: automate customer follow-up

  • Reader: a five-person service business with missed quote follow-ups.
  • Promise: build one approval-first follow-up flow and test it with sample records.
  • Evidence: screenshots of the trigger, stop conditions, approval step, and activity log.
  • Outline: map the queue, define the trigger, draft the message, add stop rules, test five cases, review the log.
  • Limitation: the system can send the wrong message when status fields are stale.
  • CTA: run the five-case test before enabling live sends.

Do this today

  1. Search one exact audience query in a private window.
  2. Record the first ten result formats and the date.
  3. Choose the format that best supports the reader's next decision.
  4. Write the evidence and limitation into the brief before drafting.
