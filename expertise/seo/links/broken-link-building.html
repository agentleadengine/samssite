Broken link building

7 min readLast checked 2026-09-28

Broken link building starts with a dead resource and a page that still cites it. You verify the error, understand why the old page was useful, and offer a relevant replacement. The editor may replace the link, remove it, choose another source, or do nothing.

The mindset

Lead with the exact broken URL and where it appears. Then explain how the proposed replacement serves the same reader task. The editor still decides whether to replace or remove the link.

The 4-step workflow

Tools for finding broken links

  • Ahrefs Site Explorer, Outbound Links, Broken. Enter a domain, see every broken outbound link.
  • Check My Links (Chrome extension). Highlights broken links on any page you visit. Good for quick spot checks on resource pages.
  • Screaming Frog. Crawl a site, export outbound links, filter for 404 responses.

Targeting strategy

  • Resource pages. Pages that exist to link to resources ("Top 25 SEO tools," "Insurance Industry Resources"). Many outbound links, some will rot over time.
  • Older guides and evergreen content. Check every outbound link before deciding whether a replacement is needed.
  • Competitor-linking sites. Sites that link to your competitors are already inclined to link to your topic. Check their outbound profiles for broken links.

The Wayback Machine trick

When you find a broken link, check Archive.org to see what the original content was. If your content is similar (or could be), your pitch gets much stronger. You can literally quote the original to show the editor you understand what was there and why it mattered.

The outreach email

Short, specific, offer value first.

Hi [Name],

Read your "Best SEO Tools of 2023" post, great list. Noticed the link to ToolX (section 4) is broken, looks like the site went offline.

I write about an SEO tool directory that covers similar tools and is actively maintained: [your URL]. Might be a useful swap for the broken link.

Either way, thought you'd want to know the link is dead.

Measure the campaign outcome

Campaign fieldHow to calculate itWhat it tells you
DeliveredCount messages accepted by the mail systemThe usable outreach base
Reply rateReplies divided by delivered messagesWhether recipients respond to the pitch
Qualified reply rateReplies that discuss the replacement divided by delivered messagesWhether the opportunity and replacement fit
Placement rateVerified replacement links divided by delivered messagesThe observed campaign outcome

Report the dated counts with each rate. Use "not applicable" when the denominator is zero. Do not turn one campaign's outcome into a promise for the next campaign.

Why it works

  • You provide real value first (fixing a broken link)
  • The ask is clear and small (swap one link for another)
  • Updating an existing post is easier than deciding whether to add something new
  • Editors appreciate the heads-up even when they don't use your replacement

Common pitfalls

  • Pitching unrelated content. Your replacement must be at least as relevant as the broken link.
  • Obviously templated emails. Personalize. Cold, robotic outreach gets deleted.
  • Asking for multiple swaps at once. One win at a time. Don't overload the email.
  • Ignoring the relationship after. A successful link swap is a warm contact. Nurture it.

Scaling carefully

Set campaign volume from the research time available. Track delivered messages, replies, qualified replies, and placed links. Follow platform rules, and ask your lawyer about the outreach laws that apply to your recipients and jurisdiction.

What to do with this

Choose a relevant resource page and verify each suspected broken destination. Pitch a replacement only when it serves the same reader task. Record delivered messages, replies, qualified replies, and verified placements. Use those counts as the campaign result, with no promised link total.

Acceptance sequence

  1. Confirm the old URL is broken from a normal browser and a header request.
  2. Read the archived page to understand why it was cited.
  3. Reject the opportunity if the replacement serves a different intent.
  4. Send one specific note that identifies the broken reference and useful replacement.
  5. Log the outcome and stop follow-up when the recipient declines.

Ask your lawyer about the outreach rules that apply to the recipient and jurisdiction.

Do this today

  1. Confirm the error and inspect the archived page.
  2. Document why the replacement fits the original citation.
  3. Log one targeted outreach outcome.

Limitation: A broken URL is not an opportunity when the replacement serves a different reader intent.
