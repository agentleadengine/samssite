Use AI inside Google Workspace or Microsoft 365

Track
Owners · lesson 11 of 16
Reading time
4 min
Last checked
28 Sep 2026
Marks
Checked My take

Your first useful AI tool may already be inside your email and documents. This lesson shows you how to test one office workflow without adding another app, login, or place for work to get lost.

Check what your office already has

Start with the account people use for work. If your team lives in Gmail and Google Docs, check Google Workspace. If it lives in Outlook, Word, and Teams, check Microsoft 365. Write down the exact plan name. Then ask the administrator whether its AI features are enabled.

Do not judge access from a screenshot on a sales page. The button can depend on the plan, account type, language, region, device, and administrator settings. Check one employee account before you promise the feature to everyone.

Google lists Workspace Business Starter at $7, Standard at $14, and Plus at $22 per user per month with an annual commitment. Enterprise pricing requires contacting sales.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 01

Microsoft lists Business Standard with Copilot at $23.50 per user per month when paid annually. Business Premium with Copilot is $32 on the same billing basis. Its Copilot Business add-on requires a qualifying Microsoft 365 plan.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Run one email workflow

Choose a message that needs a useful reply but does not involve a dispute, private record, or firm price. A request to schedule a project update works well. Give the assistant the facts it needs, the tone, and the action you want the reader to take.

In Outlook, use the Copilot control available in your licensed account. Keep the first test small enough that you can compare the draft with what you would have written.

PromptDraft a reply to this client. Use only the facts below. Keep it under 120 words. Offer Tuesday at 10:00 a.m. or Thursday at 2:00 p.m. for a 30-minute project update. Do not mention a price. End by asking which time works. Facts: the design draft is ready, the printing estimate is still pending, and Maria will join the call.

Before sending, check every name, date, time, attachment, price, and promise. In this example, the assistant must not turn a pending estimate into an approved price. It must not say the draft is final. Those are small wording changes with business consequences.

Google says Gmail AI features such as summaries, drafting, proofreading, and suggested replies require an eligible Google Workspace or Google AI plan. Availability varies by account type, language, platform, and region. On a computer, open Gmail and select Ask Gemini at the top right. Then choose a suggested prompt or write one.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 03Source 04

Turn the same facts into office work

Do not start over in every app. Reuse the approved facts from the email. Ask for a three-row task list with the task, owner, and due date. Paste that draft into a document or spreadsheet only after you confirm the assignments.

For a five-person design office, the finished list might contain three rows:

TaskOwnerDue
Send the design draftMariaTuesday, 9:00 a.m.
Confirm the printing estimateLeeWednesday, noon
Prepare the client agendaSamOne day before the meeting

The assistant can arrange the list. A person must supply the owners and dates. If those details were not in the email, the assistant should leave a blank instead of making them up.

Keep one shared prompt card for routine office work. Include the facts to use, the format to return, and the items a person must check.

Keep work inside the business account

A personal account and a company account can sit in the same browser. That makes it easy to paste work into the wrong window. Put the company profile in a separate browser profile. Ask staff to check the account name before adding customer material.

This boundary helps, but it does not make every upload appropriate. Keep passwords, payment card numbers, medical information, and full customer files out. Follow the office rule for approved data, retention, and access.

Google says Gemini in Workspace uses Workspace content to answer prompts and does not use that content to train or improve generative AI models.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 05

Microsoft says commercial Microsoft 365 Copilot prompts, responses, and Microsoft Graph data are not used to train foundation models.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 06

Where this breaks

Office AI can miss an attachment, use an old thread, or turn a suggestion into a commitment. It can also disappear from an account after a license or administrator change. Treat its output as a draft, not the office record.

If people cannot find the same feature, stop and compare their exact plans and settings. Do not buy another tool until you know whether the current account already includes the job you need.

Do this today

  1. Write down whether your office uses Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 and the exact plan.
  2. Ask the administrator which AI features are enabled for one test account.
  3. Draft one low-risk scheduling reply with the prompt above.
  4. Check every name, time, price, and promise before sending it.
