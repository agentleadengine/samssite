Five tasks to hand AI this week

Track
Owners · lesson 04 of 16
Reading time
3 min
Last checked
28 Sep 2026
Marks
Checked My take

Your first AI jobs should be useful, easy to check, and safe to redo if the draft is weak. These five are common in small offices. Use your own words and approved material, then read the result before it goes anywhere.

1. Draft a reply, do not send a reply

Give the assistant the customer message, the facts it needs, and the tone you want. Remove personal or sensitive details if you are using a personal account. Ask for a short draft, then make it sound like you.

PromptDraft a friendly reply to this customer. Thank them, answer these three points, and say what happens next. Keep it under 140 words. Do not invent a price, date, or promise. Here are the approved facts: [paste facts].

2. Summarize a thread or meeting

A long thread can hide one decision and three unanswered questions. Paste a safe version of the thread or transcript and ask for a usable handoff. This is especially helpful after a meeting when everybody remembers a different next step.

PromptSummarize this thread for the person taking over. Use four headings: decision made, open questions, promised dates, and next owner. Quote the exact wording for any date or commitment you find.

3. Turn rough notes into an SOP

An SOP is a standard operating procedure, a written set of steps someone else can follow. Start with a task you already know well, such as opening a new inquiry or preparing a routine follow-up. Dictate the messy version. The assistant can organize it, but you must correct the missing steps.

PromptTurn these rough notes into an SOP for a new office assistant. Use numbered steps, include what to check before moving on, and mark anything I need to confirm. Do not add steps that are not in my notes.

4. Draft FAQ answers from your documents

Use a current, approved document such as a policy, service sheet, or internal FAQ. Ask for customer-friendly answers based only on that material. This is better than asking the assistant to remember what your business does.

When the assistant has a source document in front of it and you ask it to stay inside that document, the review is faster and the draft is usually safer. It still needs a human read.

My takeFrom routine office document work
PromptUsing only the attached FAQ, draft answers to these five customer questions. If the document does not answer a question, write: "Needs an approved answer." Keep each response under 80 words.

5. Clean up a spreadsheet or list

AI can help make a messy column useful. Ask it to propose categories, spot duplicates, standardize labels, or explain a formula. Keep the original file. For anything tied to money, inventory, or customer records, check a sample of the results before replacing a column.

PromptI have a list with inconsistent service labels. Suggest a clean set of categories, then show a two-column mapping from each original label to its proposed category. Do not change the original data.

Current chat assistants offer features such as projects, file work, web research, and connections, but availability varies by plan and region. Start with one simple task in the interface you already have before you build a new process around a feature.

Make the first week easy to judge

For each task, save the prompt, the first draft, and the final version. You are not trying to prove that AI is magic. You are looking for a repeatable job where the first draft saves time without creating a risky new problem. If a task takes longer to explain than to do, skip it for now.

After three uses, compare the final versions. Notice which edits repeat, then add those instructions to the shared prompt.

Do this today

  1. Choose one of the five tasks that appears at least once this week.
  2. Use the matching prompt with safe, approved information.
  3. Save the final prompt in a shared list after you edit it.
  4. Ask the person who does the task whether the draft saved time, not whether it looked impressive.
