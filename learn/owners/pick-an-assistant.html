Pick one assistant for the office

Track
Owners · lesson 02 of 16
Reading time
3 min
Last checked
28 Sep 2026
Marks
Checked My take

A small office does not need four AI subscriptions and a debate every time someone has to write an email. Pick one assistant that fits the tools your team already opens all day, learn it together, and use it well before you add another.

Start with the office you already have

The best choice is usually not the assistant with the longest feature list. It is the one that sits closest to the documents, email, meetings, and spreadsheets your team already uses. That cuts down on copying and pasting, and it makes a shared habit more likely to stick.

If your office runs on Google Workspace, start by looking at Gemini in Gmail, Docs, Meet, Sheets, Drive, and NotebookLM. If it runs on Microsoft 365, look at Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams. If you use a mix of tools or mostly work in a browser, ChatGPT or Claude can be a simpler shared starting point.

Google Workspace plans list Gemini features across Gmail, Docs, Meet, Sheets, Drive, and NotebookLM. Microsoft lists Copilot business features for Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams, and other Microsoft 365 tools, subject to licensing.

Pick one default for the whole office

I would choose one default assistant for the office, even when a few people keep personal favorites. A default means the team knows where shared prompts live, who can help a new hire, and what privacy rules apply. It does not mean every person must use AI for every task.

Give the tool a real trial. Choose two people with different jobs, such as the person handling incoming requests and the person doing follow-up. Ask each to use it for the same three jobs for two weeks: draft a reply, summarize a long thread, and turn rough notes into a checklist. At the end, ask what they used without being reminded, what took too long, and what data they were nervous to paste in.

For a small office, a tool that saves ten minutes in the software people already know is more valuable than a more capable tool that stays in a browser tab nobody opens. Pick the fit first, then learn the deeper features later.

My takeFrom setting up shared office workflows

Do not decide only on a demo

A good demo makes every assistant look helpful. Test the boring work. Give each candidate the same sanitized email thread and ask for a summary with open questions. Give it a rough process and ask for an SOP. Give it an approved FAQ and ask for three customer replies. Read the results with the people who would send them.

Keep the first use low risk. No account numbers, medical details, passwords, or final pricing. Lesson 07 explains the data rules, and lesson 06 gives you the quick check before anything leaves the office.

Do this today

  1. Write down whether your office mainly uses Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, or a mix.
  2. Choose one assistant to test for two weeks, with two real office tasks.
  3. Put one safe sample email and one rough set of notes in a shared folder for the trial.
  4. Schedule a 20-minute review at the end of the two weeks and decide: keep it, switch, or stop.
