The rules that apply to you

Track

Reading time
3 min
Last checked
28 Sep 2026
Marks
Checked My take
This is not legal advice

AI rules change by place, industry, and use. This lesson gives plain facts to help you spot questions. Ask your lawyer about the rules that apply to your business, customers, and location.

The rule for a small business is not "avoid AI." It is to avoid misleading people, protect the information you handle, and know when a new law or contract may affect your use. Start with the claims you make and the decisions you let a tool influence.

The FTC still expects honest claims

The Federal Trade Commission applies existing consumer-protection law to AI claims. That means your marketing should not say an AI product guarantees growth, earnings, refunds, or accuracy unless you can support the statement. The same common-sense rule applies to AI-written copy: if your name is on it, you own the claim.

The FTC continues to apply consumer-protection law to deceptive or unsupported AI claims, including claims about business growth, earnings, refunds, and AI-detector accuracy.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 01

State rules can add duties

State laws may affect a particular kind of AI use, especially when a tool talks to consumers, makes decisions about people, or handles sensitive information. The useful first step is to write down what the tool does: drafts copy, answers customer questions, ranks applicants, summarizes calls, or recommends an action. That description gives your lawyer something concrete to assess.

Colorado SB25B-004 moved the effective date of SB24-205 to June 30, 2026. Colorado also enacted HB26-1263 with conversational AI obligations effective January 1, 2027. Confirm current agency rules before acting on implementation detail.

Changing28 Sep 2026Source 02

California's official 2026 law summary identifies AI-related measures including AB489, SB53, and SB243. The governor described SB53 as a frontier AI safeguards law. Ask counsel which, if any, apply to your company and use case.

Changing28 Sep 2026Source 03

If you serve people in the EU

The EU AI Act has dates that apply in stages. If you offer services to people in the EU, use AI in a regulated setting, or work with an EU-based customer, do not assume a US-only policy is enough. Your lawyer can help map the places you operate, the systems you use, and the role your business has in each one.

The EU AI Act entered into force August 1, 2024. Prohibitions and AI literacy applied February 2, 2025. Broader rules and transparency obligations apply from August 2, 2026, with some high-risk timelines extended by the 2026 Omnibus.

Questions to ask your lawyer

  • Which laws apply where we operate and where our customers are?
  • Do we need a disclosure when a customer is interacting with AI?
  • Which uses require human review or an appeal path?
  • What customer or employee data may enter an approved tool?
  • What records should we keep about prompts, decisions, and approvals?
  • Do our vendor contracts and privacy notice need an update?

Keep the conversation practical. Bring an example prompt, a screenshot of the workflow, the vendor's data terms, and the people affected. A vague question about "using AI" is hard to answer well.

Review this list again when the workflow changes. A tool that only drafts internal notes may have a very different risk from the same tool sending customer messages or helping make an employment decision. The details of use matter more than the name of the product.

Keep factual claims separate from your own marketing. If AI helps write a page, check that its promises match evidence you can show. Do not let a polished sentence turn a limited feature into a guarantee. Save the evidence beside the final copy.

When a rule seems unclear, pause the use and get a written answer before expanding it.

Do this today

  1. List each way your office currently uses or plans to use AI.
  2. Mark any use that speaks to customers, handles sensitive data, or affects a person.
  3. Save the vendor terms and your short description of each use.
  4. Take those materials to your lawyer before calling a workflow compliant.
