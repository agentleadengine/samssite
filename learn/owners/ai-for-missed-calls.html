AI for phones and missed calls

Track
Owners · lesson 13 of 16
Reading time
4 min
Last checked
28 Sep 2026
Marks
Checked My take

A missed call should become a clear callback, not a promise made by a machine. Start with voicemail transcription and a short queue. Keep emergencies, sensitive calls, and uncertain messages with a person.

Start with the calls you miss

Count missed calls for one week. For each message, record the caller, reason, time, and whether someone called back. Do not buy a phone agent before you know what callers are asking for.

Separate routine requests from calls that need judgment. Scheduling and quote requests can enter a normal callback queue. Complaints, payment disputes, legal questions, medical details, and safety concerns need a named person.

An auto attendant can route a caller. Voicemail transcription can make a message easier to scan. Neither tool understands your duty to the caller. Write the handoff rule before you turn either one on.

Google Voice for business starts at $10 per user per month and requires Google Workspace. Google lists auto attendants, call transfer, Calendar and Meet integration, and voicemail transcription among its business features.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 01

Turn each voicemail into one record

Use the transcript as input, not as the record itself. The callback record should contain five fields: caller, need, urgency, promised next step, and owner. Leave a field blank when the message does not support it.

PromptTurn this voicemail transcript into one callback record. Use only the caller's words. Return: caller name, callback number, reason for calling, urgency stated by the caller, promised next step, and staff owner. Write “not provided” for missing details. Do not diagnose an emergency. Do not quote or promise a price. If the transcript mentions danger, injury, a gas smell, threats, legal deadlines, medical details, or an angry dispute, write HUMAN REVIEW at the top.

Read the transcript before using the record. Listen to the audio when a name, number, address, or urgent phrase is unclear. The assistant should sort what was said. It should not decide whether the caller is safe.

Google says voicemail transcripts may be incorrect or missing.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

A twelve-call example

Imagine a two-person heating and cooling office receives 12 after-hours voicemails in one week. Four callers ask for quotes. Five want routine scheduling. Two ask about warranty work. One caller says there may be a gas smell.

The office puts the nine quote and scheduling calls into its normal callback queue. A person reviews the two warranty messages because the answer depends on the job record. The possible gas issue never enters the ordinary queue. A person listens to the recording and follows the company's emergency procedure.

The callback list might begin like this:

NeedNext stepOwner
Estimate for replacing a unitCall to schedule an inspectionOffice manager
Move Friday appointmentCheck calendar, then callScheduler
Question about prior repairOpen job record for human reviewService manager
Possible gas smellImmediate human reviewOn-call person

Notice what is missing. There is no diagnosis. There is no repair price. There is no promise that a technician will arrive at a time nobody confirmed.

Begin with transcription and callback sorting. A full AI receptionist adds more ways to make a wrong promise before the basic handoff is working.

My takeFrom designing small-office handoffs

Set the human handoff

Put a person and a response rule beside every exception. “Urgent calls go to someone” is not enough. Name the on-call role, the backup, and the channel that gets their attention.

Tell callers when a message is being recorded or transcribed according to the rules that apply to your business. Decide how long to keep audio and transcripts. Ask your lawyer about recording and consent requirements in the places where you and your callers are located.

Limit who can see voicemail text. A transcript can expose the same customer details as the recording. Remove old messages according to the office retention rule, not whenever an inbox becomes full.

Google Voice Standard includes auto attendants, AI-generated call recording, transcription, and call summaries. Google says the Workspace add-on supports unlimited users.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 03

Where this breaks

Transcription can miss an accent, a street name, a quiet phone number, or the word that changes urgency. Background noise can erase the most important sentence. A neat summary can hide that uncertainty.

Never let AI diagnose an emergency or promise a price. It should not negotiate an exception, decide whether a complaint is valid, or close a callback because the transcript looks incomplete. When the message is uncertain, a person listens and calls back.

Do this today

  1. Count last week's missed calls and label each reason.
  2. Create the five callback fields: caller, need, urgency, next step, and owner.
  3. Write the phrases and call types that require immediate human review.
  4. Test the prompt on three old voicemails and compare each result with the audio.
