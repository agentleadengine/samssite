A good request is not a clever spell. It is a short, useful brief. Give the assistant the role and context, the task, the material, and the format you need. Then improve the first draft like you would improve a note from a new teammate.

The four parts of a usable request

Role and context: say who the work is for and what is going on. The task: name the thing you want done. The materials: paste the facts, examples, or document it needs. The format: specify length, headings, table, tone, or next step.

You do not need all four in a tiny task. But when a draft misses the mark, one is usually missing. The assistant may not know your customer, may not have the actual facts, or may not know that you wanted a three-line email instead of a long explanation.

Before and after

Before: "Write an email to a customer." That leaves almost every important choice open.

Better requestYou are helping a small service office reply to an existing customer. Draft a warm email that confirms we received their request, says the next update will come by Friday, and asks for the attached photo. Use a calm, direct tone. Keep it under 120 words. Here is the customer's message: [paste it].

The better version supplies context, task, material, and format. It also gives you something easy to check: did it include the photo request, the date, and the right length?

Ask for the shape of the answer

Format is a quiet superpower. Ask for five bullets, a two-column table, a numbered SOP, or an email under 150 words. Tell it what not to do too: do not make up a price, do not use legal language, do not promise a turnaround time. A constraint often makes a draft more useful, not less.

PromptTurn these meeting notes into a handoff. Use headings for decisions, owner, due date, and open question. If a due date is not stated, write "not stated" instead of guessing. Keep the whole handoff under 250 words.

The most useful first instruction is often "If you do not have the information, say what is missing." It gives the assistant permission to stop guessing and gives you a clean place to fill the gap.

Fix a weak first answer

Do not throw away the whole conversation after one poor answer. Tell it what missed. "Too formal. Make it friendlier." "You invented a date. Use only the dates in my notes." "This is for a customer, not an internal meeting." That is faster than starting over, and it teaches you what the prompt left out.

If the answer is wrong because the source material is wrong or incomplete, stop. Add the approved facts, or ask the person who owns them. AI can rewrite a clear brief. It cannot repair a business rule nobody has decided.

ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini plan materials describe projects, files, research, and connected features, with availability varying by plan and region. A feature can help organize work, but it does not remove the need to give the assistant correct context and source material.

Keep your good prompts

When a prompt works, save it with one sentence about when to use it. Keep it in a shared document with the exact final version, not a vague reminder. Good prompts are small pieces of office process. A new hire can use them, and a busy teammate does not have to rediscover the same wording.

Give the saved prompt a quick test before calling it standard. Have another person use it with a different, safe example. If they need to explain what a blank means or which facts to paste, add that instruction to the prompt. The best shared prompts make the next right action obvious without a separate training session.

Keep a small note below the prompt with the last review date and the person responsible for the source material. That helps when a policy, price list, or customer process changes. A prompt is useful only while the facts inside it are still approved.

Do this today

  1. Choose one regular email or summary task.
  2. Write a request with all four parts: context, task, materials, and format.
  3. Review the first answer and name one specific thing to change.
  4. Save the final version of the request in your shared prompt list.
