AI meeting notes can save the typing after a call. They can also turn a misheard date into the official plan. Use the notes as a draft, get consent, and confirm every decision before the recap leaves the meeting.

Choose the meeting before the tool

Start with a routine internal or supplier meeting. Do not make a sensitive personnel, legal, medical, or customer dispute call your first test. Write down why notes are needed, who will receive them, and how long the recording or transcript will be kept.

Tell participants before recording, transcription, or AI notes begin. Explain what will be captured and where the result will go. Ask your lawyer about consent and recording requirements for the locations and work involved.

Check five things before sharing

Review the draft against the recording, transcript, or your own notes. Check names, numbers, decisions, owners, and dates. Those five items turn a readable summary into work that people may act on.

A useful recap separates three categories:

  • Confirmed decisions. The group agreed and the source supports it.
  • Assigned actions. A named person accepted a task and due date.
  • Open questions. Nobody decided, so the recap must not pretend they did.
PromptDraft a five-sentence meeting recap from these checked notes. Use three headings: Decisions, Actions, and Open questions. Give every action one named owner and one due date. If either is missing, write [OWNER NEEDED] or [DATE NEEDED]. Do not turn a suggestion into a decision. Do not add a price, promise, or deadline.

A thirty-minute supplier meeting

A small retailer finishes a 30-minute call with a packaging supplier. The AI summary lists four points. It says the next delivery is October 12, but the supplier actually said October 21. It also assigns two tasks and describes one packaging idea as approved.

The owner checks the transcript. She changes October 12 to October 21. She confirms that Lee owns the sample review by Friday. She confirms that Morgan will send the revised quantity by Monday. She moves the packaging idea into Open questions because nobody approved it.

The final recap is five sentences. It names the corrected delivery date, two owners, two due dates, and the unresolved choice. The owner sends it from the meeting chat and asks the supplier to reply if anything is wrong.

Give one person ten minutes after the meeting to approve the recap. If everybody owns the review, nobody owns the errors.

Where this breaks

A summary inherits errors from the transcript. It may miss tone, disagreement, a side conversation, or a correction made after the call. A speaker label can attach a promise to the wrong person.

Do not let the draft become the source of truth before review. Keep the recording or transcript only under the office retention rule. Limit access to the people who need the meeting record.

Do this today

  1. Pick one routine meeting and name the person who will approve its recap.
  2. Tell participants what will be captured and ask for consent before it starts.
  3. Check names, numbers, decisions, owners, and dates after the meeting.
  4. Send only confirmed actions and label every unresolved point as open.
