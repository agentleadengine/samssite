AI can turn one good idea into several marketing drafts. It cannot give your business a point of view, customer proof, or a truthful offer. You have to bring those parts first.

Start with four pieces of evidence

Open a blank note before you open the assistant. Add one question a customer actually asked. Add one fact you can prove. Add one current offer. Then add the action a reader should take.

For a local bookkeeper, the question might be, “What should I bring to a year-end cleanup?” The proof could be the firm's own document checklist. The offer could be a 20-minute intake call. The action could be a link to the booking page.

Do not ask AI to invent a testimonial, a savings figure, or a deadline. If the business did not supply the claim, the draft cannot use it. A clean sentence is not evidence.

Record customer questions after calls and appointments. That list will produce more useful marketing than asking an assistant for random post ideas.

Give the assistant a narrow job

Ask for one useful post before you ask for a week of content. Give it the source material, the reader, the format, and the words it must avoid. Tell it to mark any missing fact with brackets.

PromptWrite a Google Business Profile update for a local bookkeeping firm. Use only the notes below. Keep it under 120 words. Answer the customer question first. Use a calm, practical tone. Do not invent savings, deadlines, credentials, or testimonials. If a fact is missing, write [CHECK THIS]. End with the booking link exactly as written. Customer question: What should I bring to a year-end cleanup? Checklist: bank statements, credit card statements, payroll reports, loan statements, and the last filed return. Offer: 20-minute intake call. Booking link: https://example.com/book

The first sentence should answer the question. The checklist should match the source note. The call to action should name the 20-minute intake call without promising an outcome. Replace the example link with the firm's real booking link before posting.

Next, ask for an email and a LinkedIn post using the same approved notes. Different formats are fine. The facts must stay the same. If the draft adds “save thousands” or “avoid every penalty,” delete it because the source never proved it.

Edit until it sounds like a person

Read the draft aloud. Remove the broad opening sentence. Replace vague claims with the exact checklist, service, neighborhood, or next step. Cut any sentence the owner would never say to a customer.

Then check the business details. Confirm the service name, price, dates, phone number, hours, and link. Ask the person who owns the offer to approve it. A typo is embarrassing. A wrong price or promise costs trust.

Keep the useful answer even if the brand name disappears. “Bring your bank and credit card statements” helps the reader. “We provide comprehensive solutions for your journey” does not.

Publish one clear post

A Google Business Profile can be a useful first channel for a local business. It reaches people who are already looking at the business listing. Use the draft to answer a question, explain an offer, or announce an event.

Google Business Profile supports Update, Offer, and Event posts. A post can include text, photos or video, and an action button where available. Posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Choose the post type that matches the message. A checklist is an Update. A discount with terms is an Offer. A workshop with a date is an Event. Do not force every message into a promotion.

To publish, open the Business Profile, select Posts, select Add post, choose Update, Offer, or Event, add the details, and select Post. Google reviews published posts against its content policy.

Save the final text beside the source note and approval date. That gives the next post a reliable starting point. It also shows which claims came from the business and which words came from the assistant.

Where this breaks

AI often produces polished copy that could belong to any business. It can also invent proof, smooth over an important condition, or repeat the same rhythm in every post. More drafts do not fix weak source material.

A post may also pass your review and still fail a platform review.

Google's Business Profile posts policy tells businesses to avoid text that adds no value and auto-generated or distracting content.

Checked28 Sep 2026Source 02

Keep the post useful, specific, and readable. If it is rejected, review the policy and the actual post instead of asking AI to disguise the same message.

Do this today

  1. Write one customer question, one provable fact, one offer, and one action.
  2. Use the prompt above to draft a single post from those notes.
  3. Read it aloud and remove every unsupported or generic sentence.
  4. Confirm the details and publish only after the offer owner approves it.
