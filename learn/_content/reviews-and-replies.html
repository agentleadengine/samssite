AI can give you a calm first draft for a public review. It cannot decide what happened, approve a refund, or know which customer details must stay private. Check the record and choose the right kind of response first.

Classify the review before drafting

Put each review into one of five groups: praise, service recovery, factual correction, spam, or safety concern. The group determines what happens next.

  • Praise. Thank the reviewer and mention one detail they shared.
  • Service recovery. Acknowledge the experience and move the details to a private channel.
  • Factual correction. Check the business record before stating a narrow correction.
  • Spam. Report a policy violation instead of arguing with the account.
  • Safety concern. Save the review and send it to the person responsible for the issue.

Do not paste a customer's full record into an assistant. Use only the public review and the few confirmed facts needed for the response. Keep account numbers, health details, payment history, and private messages out.

Ask for a short first draft

Give the assistant the review type, the public text, and the confirmed response facts. Ban private details, blame, speculation, refunds, and promises. Ask for a private next step when the issue needs investigation.

PromptDraft a public reply under 80 words. Review type: service recovery. Thank the reviewer for the specific feedback. Acknowledge the late arrival without arguing about the cause. Do not include customer records, employee names, a refund, or a promise. Invite the reviewer to contact the office at 555-0100 so a person can check the booking. Use only these facts: the review says the cleaner arrived 45 minutes late, and the office has a booking record to review.

Read the draft as if the next customer will see it, because they will. Remove any sentence that tries to win the argument. Confirm the phone number and the person who will answer it. A private next step is useful only when somebody owns the follow-up.

Approved Google Business Profile replies are public, appear as the business reply, do not show the individual responder's personal name, and notify the reviewer.

Three reviews, three actions

A home-cleaning company receives three reviews in one morning. The first is a five-star note that praises careful work in the kitchen. The second reports a cleaner arriving 45 minutes late. The third names a service the company does not provide and an address outside its area.

For the first review, AI drafts a two-sentence thank-you that mentions the kitchen detail. For the second, the owner checks the booking record, confirms the late arrival, and uses the service-recovery prompt. The reply acknowledges the delay and offers the office number.

The owner does not accuse the third reviewer of lying. She searches the booking record, saves what she checked, and reports the review if it appears to violate policy. A public fight would not prove that the review belongs elsewhere.

Google lets a business report reviews that violate its policies. A review is not eligible for removal only because the business dislikes or disagrees with it.

Publish and log the service fix

In the Business Profile, open the reviews, choose the review, enter the approved reply, and publish it. Save the review type, response date, owner, and any internal service change.

The internal note matters more than a polished apology. If late arrivals keep appearing, fix scheduling or customer notification. The reply can show care. It cannot repair a process that stays broken.

Google's reply steps are: open the Business Profile, select Read reviews, select Reply beside the review, enter the response, and select Reply.

Where this breaks

AI can sound polite while admitting something the business has not confirmed. It can expose private details, invent a refund, or make every response sound identical. A polished reply cannot repair the underlying service.

Keep legal arguments, threats, employee discipline, payment details, and customer records out of public replies. Ask your lawyer about a review that raises a legal claim or creates a duty to preserve evidence.

Do this today

  1. Label three recent reviews as praise, recovery, correction, spam, or safety concern.
  2. Check the business record before giving the assistant any response facts.
  3. Draft one reply under 80 words and remove every private detail or promise.
  4. Name the person who owns the private follow-up and the service fix.
