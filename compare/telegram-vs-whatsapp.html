Telegram vs WhatsApp

The power-user and community platform against the world's default messenger.

These two solve different problems that happen to share a chat interface. WhatsApp is where your customers already are, roughly three times Telegram's user base, with default end-to-end encryption and a mature paid business messaging system run by Meta. Telegram is a broadcast and community platform first, with channels, huge groups, bots, and creator monetization that WhatsApp does not really attempt. The right question is whether you need to reach people one-to-one or build an audience.

Two things to watch on the WhatsApp side. Meta moved from per-conversation to per-message billing in mid-2025, which changed the math for high-volume senders, and most businesses buy through a solution provider that adds its own markup on top of Meta's rate card. Budget from the provider's quote, not Meta's published prices.

When to pick Telegram

Pick Telegram when you are building an audience rather than answering customers. Channels are the best broadcast product in messaging: unlimited subscribers, full-length posts, view counts, and no algorithm deciding who sees you. Pair a channel with a discussion group of up to 200,000 members and you have a community stack that WhatsApp simply cannot host at that scale.

It is also the automation platform of the two. The Bot API is open and free, so you can run support bots, payment flows, mini apps, and internal tools without per-message fees or template approvals. For developers, crypto communities, news publishers, and creators, this openness is the whole point. Premium at $4.99 a month and creator revenue sharing give heavy users and publishers a real economic layer.

The honest caveats: your audience may not be there, since Telegram's billion users cluster in specific regions and niches, and the encryption story requires trust in Telegram's servers because regular chats are not end-to-end encrypted.

When to pick WhatsApp

Pick WhatsApp when you need to reach normal people where they already are. With more than 3 billion users it is the default communication channel across Latin America, India, much of Europe, Africa, and Southeast Asia. For a local business, a WhatsApp message gets opened where an email gets ignored. The free Business app covers a small operation: catalog, quick replies, labels, and a business profile.

At scale, the Business Platform API is the mature option for transactional and marketing messaging, with verified business identity, template messages, and integrations into every major CRM. Yes, you pay per message, but you are paying for open rates that no other channel matches. And for personal and business-sensitive conversations, default end-to-end encryption on every chat is a real advantage over Telegram's opt-in model, not a marketing line.

The trade-offs are Meta's rules and Meta's rates. Marketing templates need approval, per-message costs keep rising and the pricing model keeps changing, and you build on a channel Meta fully controls, including its ad plans for the app.

What most comparisons miss

Most comparisons repeat the folk belief that Telegram is the more private app. Technically it is the opposite for everyday chats: WhatsApp end-to-end encrypts every personal conversation by default, while Telegram's regular cloud chats are encrypted only between you and Telegram's servers, meaning Telegram can read them. Telegram's privacy reputation rests on its history of resisting government requests, which is policy, not cryptography. What you are actually choosing between is trusting math plus Meta's metadata collection, or trusting one company's promises. Those are different threat models, and which one matters depends on who you are worried about.

The second miss: these are not substitutes, and the smartest operators use both. A common pattern I see is WhatsApp for one-to-one customer conversations and transactions, where reach and trust win, and Telegram for the free broadcast channel and community, where WhatsApp's group caps and per-message fees make audience building impractical. Framing it as either-or costs you the half each platform is best at.

My verdict

For business messaging to customers, WhatsApp, and it is not close: the audience is there, the API is mature, and default encryption builds trust. For communities, broadcasts, and anything bot-driven, Telegram wins on capability and cost, since its entire automation layer is free. If I had to run one playbook, I would pay WhatsApp's per-message rates for customer conversations and grow the audience on a Telegram channel in parallel. Just do not choose Telegram for privacy reasons without understanding that its default chats are not end-to-end encrypted.
