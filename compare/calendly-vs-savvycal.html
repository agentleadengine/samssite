Calendly vs SavvyCal

The default scheduler vs the challenger built around the person receiving the link. Compared on verified 2026 pricing.

Calendly and SavvyCal now cost almost the same at the entry level, ten dollars a seat, so price will not make this decision for you. What separates them is direction: Calendly has grown into team software with routing forms, round robin scheduling, and a Salesforce story, while SavvyCal keeps polishing the moment someone actually books with you. If you schedule as a team and report to a sales or ops leader, they are not really competitors. If you are one person sending links to clients, they very much are.

Pricing

Calendly has four plans. The free plan covers one event type on one connected calendar, which is genuinely usable for basic needs. Standard runs $10 per seat per month billed annually, or $12 month to month, and unlocks unlimited event types, multiple calendars, reminders, and payment collection through Stripe or PayPal. Teams is $16 per seat per month billed annually ($20 month to month) and adds round robin scheduling, lead routing, and the Salesforce integration. Enterprise starts at $15,000 per year with a 50-seat minimum.

SavvyCal keeps it to two plans. Basic is $10 per user per month with unlimited scheduling links and team scheduling. Premium is $17 per user per month and adds custom domains, delegated scheduling for assistants, and paid bookings. Annual billing gives you two months free on either plan. There is no permanent free tier, but you can set everything up before paying, and there is a 30-day money-back guarantee. I checked both vendors' pricing pages in August 2026.

When to pick Calendly

Pick Calendly when scheduling is a team function rather than a personal one. Round robin distribution, collective events, and lead routing with qualification forms are the features that matter once a sales or customer success team lives in the tool, and Calendly has invested there for years. The Teams plan connects to Salesforce, and Enterprise adds SSO, domain control, and audit logging for IT departments that require them.

Familiarity is a real advantage too. Most of the people you send a link to have used Calendly before, so nobody hesitates at the booking page. The integration catalog is the largest in the category, which matters if your stack is unusual.

The free plan is also the cheapest way to get a working scheduler. If one event type is all you need, Calendly costs nothing.

When to pick SavvyCal

Pick SavvyCal when you personally send a lot of scheduling links and you care how receiving one feels. Its signature feature is the overlay: the person booking can lay their own calendar on top of your availability and find a slot without tabbing between windows. That one feature answers the old complaint that a scheduling link pushes work onto the other person.

It also does small personal touches well. You can create single-use links, personalize a link for a specific invitee, and present preferred times without blocking the rest of your calendar. Premium adds custom domains and lets an assistant schedule on your behalf, which consultants and executives tend to use more than sales teams do.

At $10 to $17 per user it costs about the same as Calendly's paid tiers, so nobody should pick it to save money. Pick it because the booking experience is the product.

What most comparisons miss

Most head-to-heads treat these as interchangeable schedulers with different polish, then rank them on feature counts. The more useful frame is where each roadmap is going. Calendly is building the meeting layer for go-to-market teams: routing, qualification, CRM sync, and now AI add-ons like Notetaker. SavvyCal is building for the individual sender, and its best features, overlays, single-use links, and delegation, barely register in a team evaluation matrix.

Also worth naming: switching costs here are low. Your data is mostly event types and integration settings, not years of records. You can start on one, change your mind in an afternoon, and lose very little. I would not agonize over this choice the way I would over a CRM.

My verdict

For a team with routing needs, a Salesforce requirement, or an IT security checklist, I would pick Calendly and not think twice. For a solo consultant or a small firm where the scheduling link is part of the client experience, I lean SavvyCal: the overlay genuinely removes friction and the price difference is trivial. I would only stay on Calendly's free plan if one event type covers you, and revisit once you are paying either way.
