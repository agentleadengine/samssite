Zoom vs Google Meet

A dedicated meetings product against the one bundled with Google Workspace, compared on price, limits, and fit.

Zoom is still the default external meeting tool in many industries, and its paid plans stretch to 30-hour meetings, participant caps in the hundreds, and a deep add-on catalog for webinars and events. Google Meet's pitch is different: if your team already pays for Google Workspace, Meet is effectively free, lives inside Gmail and Calendar, and covers recording, larger calls, and Gemini AI on the higher tiers. The real comparison is rarely feature against feature. It is whether a separate meetings bill earns its keep next to a suite you already pay for.

Pricing

Zoom's free Basic plan caps group meetings at 40 minutes with up to 100 participants, and gives you a small taste of AI Companion: three meeting summaries, three note-takings, and three in-meeting questions. Pro removes the pain points with 30-hour meetings and unlimited AI Companion; Business raises the cap to 300 participants and Enterprise goes past 1,000. Zoom no longer prints dollar amounts on parts of its pricing page and third-party trackers disagree on the current list, so I will state it as a range: Pro runs roughly $13 to $17 per user per month depending on billing term, with Business a few dollars more per seat and Enterprise quote-based.

Google Meet has no standalone business price; you buy Google Workspace. Starter lists at $7 per user per month on an annual commitment, Standard at $14, and Plus at $22, and Google runs periodic promotions on those list prices, so check the current Workspace pricing page before budgeting (this page was checked in August 2026). Every paid tier allows 24-hour meetings. Standard adds recording and a 150-participant cap, Plus raises it to 500, Enterprise to 1,000. On a free personal Google account, group calls cap at 60 minutes and 100 participants, though 1:1 calls can run up to 24 hours.

The comparison that matters: a 10-person team on Workspace Standard pays about $140 a month at list price and gets email, storage, Docs, and Meet with recording. Adding Zoom Pro for the same ten people costs roughly $130 to $170 a month on top, for meetings alone.

When to pick Zoom

Pick Zoom when meetings are how you make money. Sales calls, client workshops, webinars, and trainings lean on things Zoom simply does better or does at all: dedicated webinar and events products, mature breakout rooms, granular host controls, and a 30-hour ceiling nobody hits by accident. The third-party app marketplace also matters if your CRM or notetaker needs to sit inside the meeting.

There is also the soft factor I would not dismiss: clients expect a Zoom link. The client-side experience is a known quantity on every device and corporate network, and nobody on a sales call wants to spend the first five minutes debugging a meeting tool. If your counterparties skew corporate, Zoom is the path of least resistance.

The trade-off is a real per-seat bill for something your office suite may already include, and free-tier limits (40 minutes) that push you to pay sooner than Meet does.

When to pick Google Meet

Pick Meet when your team already runs on Google Workspace and most meetings are internal. Every calendar invite gets a Meet link automatically, calls open in the browser with no client install, and admin, billing, and security live in the console you already manage. For that team, choosing Meet is less a purchase than a default, and the marginal cost is zero.

The product itself stopped being the weak option a while ago. Paid tiers run 24-hour meetings, Standard and up record to Drive, Plus handles 500 participants, and Gemini features are folded into Workspace plans rather than sold as a separate add-on. For standard meetings, most teams will not find a practical gap.

Where Meet runs out of road is the event end of the spectrum: there is no true webinar product, and host controls are thinner than Zoom's. If you run paid trainings or large external events, Meet alone will feel tight.

What most comparisons miss

The bundle asymmetry is the whole game. Zoom's price is a real line item; Meet's price is usually zero on the margin because it rides on a Workspace subscription bought for email and Docs. Comparing sticker prices head to head misses this: the honest question is what Zoom adds over the Meet you already own, and whether that delta is worth $13 to $17 per seat per month.

Free tiers punish different users. Zoom's 40-minute cap lands mid-conversation on exactly the discovery calls freelancers use the free plan for; Meet's 60-minute group cap is more forgiving, and 1:1 calls can run up to 24 hours. For a solo consultant not yet paying for either, that 20-minute difference is the practical distinction.

And the AI story has quietly converged. Zoom includes unlimited AI Companion on paid plans at no extra charge, and Google folded Gemini into Workspace plans. Two years ago meeting AI was an upsell on both sides; today it is table stakes, so I would not let either vendor's AI marketing drive the decision.

My verdict

For a team already paying for Google Workspace with mostly internal meetings, I pick Meet and keep the money. It does the job, and the calendar integration removes real friction. I pick Zoom when meetings are the product: client calls, webinars, trainings, anything where long sessions, event tooling, or the polish clients expect actually earns revenue. Plenty of small firms land on the hybrid I usually suggest: Workspace for the suite, one or two Zoom Pro seats for client-facing work. That costs far less than licensing Zoom for everyone and matches how the tools actually get used.
