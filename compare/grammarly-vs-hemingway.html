Grammarly vs Hemingway

An always-on grammar checker against a deliberate readability editor, with verified 2026 pricing.

Grammarly and Hemingway solve different problems, and most comparisons force a winner anyway. Grammarly is an always-on checker that follows you into email, documents, and the browser, with AI drafting bolted on. Hemingway is an editor you paste a finished draft into so it can flag dense sentences, passive voice, and filler. I treat Grammarly as insurance against errors and Hemingway as a deliberate editing pass, and the pricing gap between them is bigger than most people expect.

Pricing

Grammarly's numbers, checked against its plans page in 2026: Free costs nothing and includes core grammar and spelling checks plus 100 AI prompts a month. Pro runs $12 per member per month when billed annually, which is $144 a year. Paying month to month costs $30, so the annual commitment amounts to a 60 percent discount. Pro adds full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustment, plagiarism and AI-text detection, and 2,000 AI prompts per member per month. Enterprise is custom priced and adds SSO, data controls, and unlimited AI prompts.

Hemingway is far cheaper. The web editor is free with no account. Hemingway Editor Plus, which adds AI fixes for the issues it highlights, starts at $100 per year ($8.33 a month) or $10 billed monthly, with a two-week trial. Team plans allot 10,000 AI sentences per user per month. There is also a classic desktop app sold as a one-time purchase of about $20, useful if you want offline editing without a subscription.

The practical takeaway: a year of Grammarly Pro costs more than a decade of Hemingway's desktop app. That only makes sense if you use what Pro adds.

When to pick Grammarly

Pick Grammarly when your writing is scattered across tools all day: email, CRM notes, support replies, documents, chat. Its value is coverage. The browser extension and desktop apps catch errors in the moment, in whatever field you happen to be typing into, and that is something Hemingway does not attempt.

It is also the right pick for teams that need consistency. Pro and Enterprise support style guides and brand tones, and the plagiarism and AI-detection checks matter for agencies and content teams shipping work under their own name. The free tier is genuinely useful, so I would start there and only upgrade once you find yourself wanting the rewrite and tone features several times a week.

When to pick Hemingway

Pick Hemingway when the problem is not typos but bloat. Paste in a draft and it grades the reading level, highlights sentences that are hard to follow, and flags passive voice and adverbs in color. It gives you a concrete editing task list instead of inline autocorrect. For blog posts, landing pages, and cold emails, that pass is worth more than another grammar check.

It is also the budget pick by a wide margin. The web editor is free, the desktop app is a small one-time purchase, and the Plus subscription costs a third of Grammarly Pro. There is a quieter benefit too: because Hemingway shows you the pattern behind each highlight, it tends to train the habit out of you. Grammarly fixes your sentence; Hemingway nudges you to write it tighter the first time.

What most comparisons miss

These tools disagree about what good writing is. Grammarly optimizes for correct and polished, and if you accept every suggestion, prose drifts toward a safe, uniform register. Hemingway optimizes for short and blunt, and taken literally it can flatten nuance. Both are advisors, not authorities, and the skill is knowing when to ignore each one.

Privacy is the other quiet difference. Grammarly is a cloud service, so your text is processed on its servers, which some employers restrict. Hemingway's desktop app works entirely offline, which makes it the safer choice for sensitive drafts.

Finally, almost nobody mentions that the free tiers cover most people. Grammarly Free catches the embarrassing mistakes, and Hemingway's free editor does everything except auto-fix. I would exhaust both before paying for either.

My verdict

Use both, in different roles. Keep Grammarly's free tier running everywhere as error insurance, and run finished drafts through Hemingway's free editor before publishing. If you pay for one, match it to your actual bottleneck: pay for Grammarly Pro if your risk is mistakes at volume across a team, pay for Hemingway Plus if your risk is wordy drafts nobody finishes reading. For my own writing, the Hemingway pass changes more.
