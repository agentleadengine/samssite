Ahrefs vs Semrush

The two flagship SEO toolkits, compared on what they actually cost and do in 2026.

Both tools cover the core SEO jobs: keyword research, backlink analysis, rank tracking, and site audits. The real decision axis is scope. Ahrefs is a focused SEO tool with the best backlink index in the business, while Semrush is a broader marketing suite that folds in PPC research, social scheduling, and content tools. If SEO is your whole job, the choice looks different than if SEO is one of five things you handle.

Watch the seat count. Every Ahrefs plan below Enterprise includes one user, and Semrush charges around $45 a month and up per additional user. For a three-person team the sticker price is not the real price on either tool.

When to pick Ahrefs

Pick Ahrefs if backlinks are central to your work. If you build links for clients, audit toxic link profiles, or do competitive gap analysis on referring domains, the depth and freshness of the Ahrefs index still shows. The interface is also cleaner and faster. I can pull a competitor snapshot in Ahrefs in a couple of minutes without wading through modules I never use.

The $29 Starter tier matters more than people admit. If you are a solo consultant or a small business owner who checks rankings and spies on a few competitors, Starter covers a surprising amount of that for a quarter of the entry price of either flagship plan. And Ahrefs Free gives you real data on your own verified site at no cost, which is the honest way to start.

The tradeoff is that anything beyond core SEO costs extra. Content tools and AI visibility tracking are paid add-ons, and every plan below Enterprise is single-user. Ahrefs is priced like a specialist tool because it is one.

When to pick Semrush

Pick Semrush if you run paid and organic together. Its PPC research is far ahead of anything Ahrefs offers: competitor ad copy, ad spend estimates, and keyword-level CPC data in the same workspace as your organic research. If you manage Google Ads and SEO for the same sites, that alone usually settles it.

It also fits generalist marketers better. Content templates, an SEO writing assistant, position tracking with daily updates, and AI prompt tracking are included in the plans rather than sold as add-ons. Semrush restructured its lineup in the last year around plans named SEO, Starter, Pro+, and Advanced, and the mid tiers now undercut the equivalent Ahrefs plans on sticker price.

The cost is complexity. Semrush has a lot of surface area, and if you only need keywords and backlinks you will pay for modules you never open. Budget time to learn where things live.

What most comparisons miss

Most comparisons treat this as a data-quality contest, and the data has largely converged. For typical keyword and rank tracking work, both tools will give you answers that lead to the same decisions. Where they have not converged is pricing architecture. Ahrefs sells a lean core and monetizes everything else as add-ons. Semrush bundles more into each plan but charges hard for seats. Which model is cheaper depends entirely on your team shape, so price out your actual seat count and add-on needs before comparing headline numbers.

The other thing worth watching in 2026 is that both companies are racing to sell AI search visibility, meaning how often your brand appears in ChatGPT and AI Overview style answers. Semrush includes AI prompt tracking in its plans, while Ahrefs sells Brand Radar from $199 a month. If AI visibility reporting is becoming part of your client deliverables, that difference changes the math quickly.

My verdict

Both are good tools and neither is a mistake. I lean Ahrefs for pure SEO work because the backlink index, the speed, and the $29 Starter tier fit how consultants and specialists actually operate. I lean Semrush the moment paid search enters the picture, because running PPC research in a separate tool is worse than any weakness Semrush has. If you are a one-person marketing department doing a bit of everything, Semrush earns its bundle. Just price your real seat count on both before you commit annually.
