Klaviyo vs Mailchimp

Ecommerce-native email marketing vs the general-purpose default, compared on pricing, segmentation, and where each one actually pays off.

The real decision axis here is not features, it is whether your revenue comes from an online store. Klaviyo is built around ecommerce data: it syncs order history and browsing behavior from Shopify or WooCommerce and lets you segment and trigger on it. Mailchimp is a general-purpose email tool that is cheaper at small list sizes and easier for plain newsletters. If you run a store, the pricing gap usually pays for itself. If you do not, you are paying Klaviyo prices for features you will never use.

Pricing

Both platforms price by list size, but they count differently, and that difference matters more than the sticker prices. These are the published numbers as of mid-2026.

Two catches. Mailchimp charges for every contact in your audience, including people who unsubscribed, and the same person on two audiences counts twice. Klaviyo only bills active profiles. So a Mailchimp list that looks cheap on paper can quietly grow expensive if you never prune it. On the other side, Klaviyo's price climbs steadily with profile count and there is no cheaper tier to retreat to, since every plan includes everything.

When to pick Klaviyo

Pick Klaviyo if you run an online store and email is a revenue channel, not just a communication channel. The core value is that your store's order and browsing data lives inside your email tool. You can build a segment like "bought twice, has not purchased in 90 days, viewed a product this week" and trigger a flow from it. Abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase, and winback flows are the standard playbook, and Klaviyo makes them straightforward because the data is already there.

It also makes sense once you care about attribution. Klaviyo ties revenue to specific flows and campaigns, so you can see what a welcome series actually earns. For a DTC brand doing meaningful volume, that reporting alone usually justifies the price gap over Mailchimp. The predictive features, like expected next order date and churn risk, are useful at scale, though I would not buy the platform for those alone.

The honest caveat: Klaviyo costs more at almost every list size, and the cost grows with your list whether or not you email it well. It rewards operators who prune inactive profiles and actually build flows. If you plan to send one monthly blast, you are overpaying.

When to pick Mailchimp

Pick Mailchimp if you are not an ecommerce business, or your list is small and your needs are simple. A service business, a nonprofit, a local shop sending a monthly update: Mailchimp does that job well for less money. Essentials at $13/mo is a perfectly good newsletter tool, and the editor is friendlier for someone who does not live in marketing software.

It is also the reasonable choice under roughly 2,500 contacts even for stores. At that size, the revenue lift from Klaviyo's deeper segmentation rarely covers the price difference, and Mailchimp's basic abandoned-cart and store integrations are adequate. You can migrate later; list exports are routine and most stores switch to Klaviyo once flows start driving real revenue.

Watch the tier gating, though. Mailchimp's headline prices are for lower tiers, and features like advanced automation and better analytics push you toward Standard or Premium. Price the tier you will actually need, not the one in the ad.

What most comparisons miss

Most comparisons frame this as cheap vs powerful, but the billing models are the real trap. Mailchimp billing all contacts including unsubscribes means an unmanaged list drifts upward in cost forever, while Klaviyo's active-profile billing quietly rewards list hygiene. I have seen Mailchimp accounts paying for thousands of dead contacts simply because nobody archived them. Whichever tool you choose, the discipline of pruning matters more than the platform's rate card.

The other thing that gets missed is switching cost direction. Moving from Mailchimp to Klaviyo is easy and common. Moving the other way is rare and painful, because once your flows depend on store-behavior triggers, no general-purpose tool can reproduce them. That asymmetry means starting on Mailchimp is a low-risk default, and starting on Klaviyo is a commitment you should only make once ecommerce email is clearly your growth lever.

My verdict

If you run a store doing more than a few thousand dollars a month, pick Klaviyo and build the standard flows. The price gap is real, but revenue attribution and behavior-based triggers earn it back. If you are not an ecommerce business, Klaviyo is the wrong tool at any price, and Mailchimp Essentials or Standard is the sensible choice. Under about 2,500 contacts, start on Mailchimp either way and switch when flows become your bottleneck. Whatever you pick, prune your list. The billing model punishes hoarders on both platforms, just at different speeds.
