Run the way you would want your own house painted.

True Coat Painting started with a simple annoyance: paint jobs that looked great on move-out day and rough a year later, because nobody scraped the loose stuff or gave the primer time to do its job. The idea was to run the job the other way around. Slow at the start, so the finish holds.

On most jobs, the same people who walk the house and write the quote are the ones on the ladder. There is no separate sales visit and a separate crew who never met each other. If the plan changes once the drop cloths are down, whoever quoted the job is there to change it.

The work is not glamorous. It is measuring twice, taping straight, and coming back to check the color in daylight before calling it done.

How the business works.

Four steps, in the same order on every house, so you know which one we are on when you get home.

  1. 01Walkthrough and written quote

    We measure the rooms, check the surfaces, and talk through sheen and color. The quote lists what gets prepped, what gets painted, and what it costs, before anything is decided.

  2. 02Protect and prep

    Floors and furniture covered, hardware off, holes filled, old caulk cut out and replaced. This is the longest part of the job, and the reason the finish holds up.

  3. 03Prime and paint

    Spot- or full-prime depending on the surface, then two finish coats. A wet edge, sanding between coats on trim, and a ventilated room while it dries.

  4. 04Walk it together

    Plastic comes up, outlets and switch plates go back on, the crew vacuums, and we walk every room with you in daylight before calling it finished.

Where the crew works.

Across Long Island, NY, in houses that are lived in and houses sitting empty between owners. If you are near the edge of the area, call and ask. We would rather say no on the phone than waste your afternoon.
