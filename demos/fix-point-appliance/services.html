Six appliances, and the symptoms we hear most

Most major brands, including front-load, stackable, and built-in units. Tell us the symptom and the model number and we know what to load before we leave the shop.

Refrigerators & Freezers

Cooling & ice

Not cooling, freezing food on the fresh side, water pooling under the crisper, an ice maker that stopped filling, or a compressor that never shuts off — these are the calls we get most, and most of them are a single part.

When to call: the compressor's running non-stop or the fresh side is icing over — call before the food goes.

Washers

Drain & spin

Won't drain, won't spin, walks across the floor on the spin cycle, leaks at the door seal, or stops mid-cycle with an error code on the panel.

When to call: an error code isn't a reason to replace it — most panels are telling you exactly which part failed.

Dryers

Heat & drum

No heat, three cycles to dry one load, a drum that won't turn, or a squeal that gets louder. We check the vent run while we're there, since that causes about half of it.

When to call: if drying time has crept up over months, it's usually the vent, not the machine.

Dishwashers

Drain & leaks

Standing water in the tub, dishes coming out gritty, a soap door that won't open, or a slow leak showing up at the front kick panel.

When to call: standing water after a full cycle almost always means the drain, not the wash.

Ovens & Ranges

Temp & ignition

An oven that won't hold temperature, a burner that clicks without lighting, a bake element that burned through, or a door that stopped sealing shut.

When to call: a burner that clicks but won't catch is usually a five-minute fix, not a new range.

Over-the-Range Microwaves

Built-in units

Turntable stuck, runs but doesn't heat, a door latch failure that kills the whole unit, or a vent fan that stopped pulling steam off the range.

When to call: a dead latch disables the whole unit as a safety lock — that's a part, not a replacement.

How a visit works

Four steps, and none of them involve guessing.

1

Call with the model number

It's on a sticker inside the door, behind the crisper drawer, or along the door frame.

2

Pick an arrival window

Two-hour windows, morning or afternoon. You get a text when the truck's on the way.

3

Diagnosis first, price second

The diagnostic is $89. Approve the repair and it comes off the total. Don't, and that's all you owe.

4

Fixed on the spot when we can

Belts, pumps, igniters, thermostats, and door seals ride on the truck. Anything ordered gets scheduled the day it lands.

Not every repair is worth making

When the part and labor add up to more than about half of a comparable new unit, we say so before we order anything — so that money can go toward the replacement instead.

Know what's wrong before you spend a dollar on it.

Two minutes on the phone usually gets you a window and a straight answer.
