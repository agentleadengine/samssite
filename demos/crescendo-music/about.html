About the studio

Crescendo started with a piano teacher, a spare room, and a handful of students whose parents wanted something specific: a lesson that showed up on the same afternoon every week and built toward an actual piece of music, not just scales.

The studio has grown to cover guitar, violin, and voice, but the idea hasn't changed. Every student — six years old or sixty — gets a plan for the term: a piece worth working toward, at a pace that fits them, with practice notes sent home after every lesson so nothing gets lost between weeks.

Every June, anyone who wants to is welcome to play a piece at the studio's recital for family and friends. Plenty of students never do, and that's fine too — it has never been a requirement.

How a term starts

The same four steps for every new student, whatever the instrument.

I

A phone call

Tell us the instrument, the student's age, and which afternoons work. We'll tell you honestly what's open.

II

An intro lesson

A full-length first lesson, so the teacher and student can get a feel for each other and hear where things stand.

III

A weekly slot

Same day, same time, every week for the term, so it lands in the family's schedule and stays there.

IV

A piece to work toward

Teacher and student choose a goal piece together. Practice notes go home after every lesson.

Where lessons happen

Lessons are held at the studio in Long Island, NY. In-home lessons are available in parts of Nassau and western Suffolk when the schedule allows, with a small travel charge added to the weekly rate.

Weekly private lessons start at $45 for a half hour. Terms run September through June, with a shorter summer session.
