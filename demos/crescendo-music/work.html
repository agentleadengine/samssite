What a term actually produces

Six sample projects, one per instrument track — the kind of thing a term of weekly lessons builds toward. Drawn, not photographed: a real studio wouldn't publish a student's name or face, so consider these a fair sketch of the work itself.

Sample project — Piano

A first piece worth showing off

A method-book beginner spends the fall on hand position and reading, then picks a real piece for spring — something they chose, not something assigned.

Sample project — Guitar

Two weeks to a strum pattern

Most guitar students are playing a full song's chord progression by the second lesson. From there it's fingerstyle, barre chords, and whatever's on their playlist.

Sample project — Violin

Bow hold, then the audition

Posture and bow hold come first, slowly. For students aiming at a school orchestra audition, the excerpt gets built one phrase at a time.

Sample project — Voice

A set list, one song at a time

Breath support and diction get taught through songs the student actually wants to sing, until there's a small set list ready to perform.

Sample project — Music theory

Reading the key signature

Key signatures, intervals, and ear training — usually added alongside an instrument, sometimes taken on its own for a student headed into a placement test.

Sample project — Adult beginners

Starting over, at your own pace

No recital requirement, no homework unless it's asked for. Just a weekly half hour and a piece worth coming back for.

Ready to start your own?

A phone call is the first step. Tell us the instrument and the student's age and we'll take it from there.
