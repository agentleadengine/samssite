A small shop, on purpose

Why The Soapy Paw stays small, how a normal week runs, and where it serves dogs across Long Island.

The Soapy Paw started the way a lot of small shops do: grooming friends' and neighbors' dogs at the kitchen sink before it was ever a business. What stood out was how differently those dogs acted compared to a trip to a big grooming counter. No crate wall, no dryer cage, no waiting their turn next to four other nervous dogs. Just one dog, one groomer, and enough time to get it right.

That's still how the shop runs. One dog on the table at a time. Hand-drying instead of cage dryers, because a warm cage left running is fast but it isn't gentle. If a coat shows up matted, the answer is a phone call and a real conversation about the options, not a clipper going in without asking first.

Most days are a mix: a few full grooms booked in advance, a couple of bath and brush appointments, and a run of walk-in nail trims before eleven. Call ahead and describe your dog, breed, coat, anything they're nervous about, and the shop will tell you what a visit would look like before you book anything.
