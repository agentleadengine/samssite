About Summit

Summit started with one kid at a kitchen table and a math worksheet that wasn't making sense. Renee Marsh was spending her mornings in a classroom of thirty and her afternoons wondering what would happen if she could work with just one student at a time, long enough to find out where the trouble actually started. So she did. That's still the whole method.

Word got around Long Island the slow way, one family telling another. As Summit grew, Renee brought on a small group of tutors she trains and pairs with each family herself. The rule never changed: your student keeps the same tutor from September to June. Nobody hands your kid off to whoever's free that week.

If a student needs something Summit can't give, like an evaluation, a reading specialist, or honestly just more sleep and fewer activities, we'll say so instead of selling more sessions. That's an easier promise to keep when the tutor sitting across the table is the same person who'll be there in March.

Renee Marsh

How the business works

Four steps, no contract.

1

A phone call

Ten minutes. Tell us the class, the grade so far, and what you've already tried.

2

First session and a check

The tutor works with your student and finds where the ground actually gets soft.

3

A plan you can read

A short written plan: what we'll cover, how often, and what to expect by when.

4

Weekly sessions

Same tutor, same time slot. A note after every session, and we adjust when the class does.

Start with a phone call.

Ten minutes usually gets you a next step, not a sales pitch.
