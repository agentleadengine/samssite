Everything that keeps a driveway flat and dry

If it's asphalt and it's failing, it's probably on this list. We'll tell you straight if a driveway needs the full tear-out or if a cheaper fix buys you a few more seasons.

Driveway paving

A full tear-out and repave when the base underneath has failed, or a fresh overlay when the base is still sound and just needs new asphalt on top. Either way: graded and compacted right, laid and rolled in the correct lifts, and edges cut clean against the garage and the walk instead of feathered to a fade.

Good to know

We check the base before quoting the repave — a phone estimate can't tell you what's underneath.

When you need it

Alligator-cracking across the whole surface, birdbaths that never drain, or sinking near the garage.

Sealcoating

A protective coat that slows down what sun, salt and oil do to asphalt over a season. Cracks get filled first, the surface is cleaned and degreased, then the coat goes on squeegeed flat and left to cure before anyone drives on it. Brings the black back instead of leaving it grey.

Good to know

Needs a dry stretch and warm enough weather — we won't coat a driveway that's about to get rained on.

When you need it

The black has faded to grey, or hairline cracks are just starting to show.

Crack filling

Cracks routed out, cleaned, and filled with hot rubberized filler leveled flush with the surface — before water gets underneath and turns one crack into a pothole. Usually a same-day fix, and a good stopgap if a full repave isn't in the budget yet.

Good to know

Filler needs to cure before you drive over it — we'll tell you the window before we leave.

When you need it

Any crack wider than a pencil, especially ones running toward the edge of the slab.

Aprons & extensions

The apron where the driveway meets the street takes the hardest beating on the whole slab — plow blades, delivery trucks, the school bus turning around. We tie a new apron into the existing pitch so it doesn't look bolted on, or extend a driveway for a third car, a boat, or a trailer.

Good to know

An extension gets graded to match your existing driveway's pitch, not just poured flat beside it.

When you need it

A cracked or sunken apron at the street, or you need a wider pad without repaving the whole driveway.

Parking pads

A standalone pad off to the side for a boat, an RV, a trailer, or just an extra car — built with a base sized for the load it'll actually carry, graded to drain away from the house, and a clean cut edge instead of asphalt trailing off into the lawn.

Good to know

Tell us what's parking on it — a boat trailer and a sedan need different base depth.

When you need it

Whatever's parking on the grass right now is turning it to mud.

Not sure which one you need?

Call anyway. Most driveways are some mix of these, and we'll sort out which ones apply once we've actually looked at it.
