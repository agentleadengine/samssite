Two guys, one paver, one roller.

My brother and I both ran crews for bigger paving outfits before we started Blacktop Brothers — him mostly on the roller, me mostly quoting and running the plant orders. What we kept running into was the same thing: a company big enough to have a sales team is usually too big to have the same guy quote it and pave it. We wanted to be small enough that those are the same two people.

Most jobs start with a call and a rough description, but the actual number comes from walking the driveway — checking the base, the drainage, how bad the cracking really is. A phone quote on asphalt is a guess dressed up as a number, and we'd rather give you a real one a day later than a wrong one right now.

There's no lot full of trucks and no dispatcher between you and the crew. One of us answers the phone, and if it's not a good time, we call back the same day — usually with a better answer than we'd have given standing in your driveway anyway.

What a normal call sounds like

You describe what you're seeing — cracking, a low spot, asphalt gone grey — and we ask enough questions to know whether it's a full repave, an overlay, or something smaller. We'll come look before we put a number on it, and if the weather's not going to cooperate on the day we planned, you hear that from us ahead of time, not as a truck that never shows.
