Everything that turns a slab into a showroom.

Five services, one system. Prep and coatings are where the floor gets its strength; flake is where it gets its look; cabinets and outdoor slabs round out the bay.

Epoxy Coatings

This is the base system — the part that actually bonds to your slab and does the structural work. Everything else, including the flake, sits on top of it.

It goes down as a full-strength, two-part epoxy, rolled edge-to-edge in one continuous pour so there's no seam where one day's work stopped and the next started.

  • Two-part epoxy, not a single-part "garage paint" that peels under tires
  • Color coat picked to match or set off your flake blend
  • Rolled edge-to-edge in one pour, no seam lines
  • Built to hold up to chemicals, hot tires, and dropped tools
When you need it

Any bare, painted, or peeling garage floor that's never had a real coating system. It's also the right first call before flake, cabinets, or a patio extension go in.

Flake Finishes

This is the step most people are actually picturing when they say "epoxy floor." Flake goes down broadcast, full coverage, into the wet base coat — the look that makes the garage feel like showroom floor instead of storage.

You're not picking a blend off a screen. We bring samples into your bay and let your own garage light make the call.

  • Full broadcast or accent-blend flake, your call
  • Blend samples brought to your garage, viewed under your own light
  • Clear topcoat sealing the flake in for good
  • UV-stable topcoat option for patio extensions
When you need it

For the showroom look — it hides minor slab imperfections and everyday dust between cleanings, and it's the finish that gets photographed.

Concrete Prep & Repair

Nobody asks about this layer, but it's the one that decides whether the coat holds for years or lifts in a season. Diamond grinding opens the concrete so the epoxy has something to actually grab.

Cracks, spalling, and any old coating that's flaking get dealt with here — before anything new goes down on top of them.

  • Diamond grinding to open the concrete's surface
  • Crack and spall repair before the coat goes down
  • Moisture check on the slab
  • Oil, paint, and old coating removal
When you need it

Almost always — it's underneath every job we do. Older concrete, a prior coating, or any visible cracking make it non-negotiable, not optional.

Cabinets & Storage

A showroom floor still needs somewhere for everything that used to live on it. Slatwall panels go up around the bay, sized to fit what you actually store, with hooks and bins arranged to match.

Base cabinets come in where floor storage still makes more sense than wall storage — a workbench run, a tool chest bay, whatever the space calls for.

  • Wall-mounted slatwall panels sized to your bay
  • Hooks, bins, and shelf brackets arranged around what you store
  • Base cabinets where floor storage beats wall storage
When you need it

Add it once the floor's done, or plan it in from the start if the garage has always been more storage unit than showroom.

Patios & Basements

The same coat system, built for slabs outside the garage door or below it. Outdoor and below-grade concrete each need their own version of the system, not just a matching color rolled on top.

Patios get a UV-stable topcoat so sun exposure doesn't yellow or chalk the finish. Basements get a moisture check before anything goes down, since below-grade slabs move differently than a garage floor.

  • The same coat system extended out the garage door onto a patio slab
  • UV-stable topcoat built for sun exposure
  • Basement-rated system with a moisture check first
When you need it

Once the garage is done and the slab keeps going — through the door onto a patio, or down a staircase into a finished basement.
