The short version
of how Torque runs.

Not a franchise, not a corporate shop. Here's who's behind the counter and how a repair actually goes, start to finish.

How it started

Dana didn't plan on running a shop. It started in a driveway: a neighbor's alternator, a coworker's brake job, a check engine light three other guesses had already gotten wrong. Word gets around fast when somebody actually explains what's broken instead of just fixing it and handing over a bill.

That's the part that carried over into Torque. Say what's wrong, say what it costs, then wait for a yes before anybody picks up a wrench. It's slower than just doing the job. It's also the only way you actually know what you're paying for.

Now it's a real bay instead of a driveway, but the order hasn't changed. Tell us what the car's doing, we look at it and call you back, and nothing gets replaced that you didn't agree to first.

Long Island drivers, mostly.

Commuters, contractors, first cars, and the family minivan that's been through three kids. Pre-purchase inspections done while you wait most weekday mornings, so you know what you're buying before you hand anyone a check.

Three steps. No surprises.

This is the order every job follows, from a check engine light to a full brake job.

1

Tell us what it's doing

Noise, smell, warning light, or just a feeling that something changed. Drop it off or call and describe it. The details you notice while driving save us diagnostic time.

2

We diagnose and call you

You get a phone call with the cause, the parts, the labor and the total. If there's a cheaper way to get you safely down the road, that's part of the same call.

3

Nothing happens until you say go

We don't start work you didn't approve, and we don't add line items at pickup. If we find something extra once we're in there, you get another call first.
