A house is a very large circuit.

Ampere House started from a pattern Nate kept running into: houses where the electrical had been added onto in pieces, a circuit here, an outlet there, until nobody, including the last electrician through, could say for sure what fed what. Every fix turned into a guess until someone opened the panel and started testing.

So the approach here starts before any tool comes out of the truck: walk the house, work out what the panel actually needs to carry, and draw the plan on paper. Circuits get named before they get pulled, not after. Fixtures get placed where the room needs light, not wherever the framing happens to make it easy.

The name's a small joke that stuck: a house is really just a very large circuit, and getting the amperage right for it is most of the job. The rest is testing what you built instead of hoping it works, and being straight with the people living there about what it actually needed.

How a job goes

The same four steps whether it's one stubborn outlet or a whole rewire.

1

Describe the job

Call and explain what's going on or what you're planning. You'll get a straight read on whether it's a quick fix or a bigger plan.

2

Walk-through

For anything beyond a repair, Nate walks the house, checks the panel, and works out the actual load before anything's scheduled.

3

The work, scheduled

Circuits get pulled, the panel gets set, fixtures go in, on a day that's actually on the calendar, not "sometime this week."

4

Tested and signed off

Every circuit gets checked with a meter before the job's called done, and the panel's left ready for inspection.
