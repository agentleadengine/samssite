Five things we handle, each planned before it's priced.

Whole-home electrical work, from a single stubborn circuit to a full panel change. Every job on this page starts the same way: a walk-through, a real plan, then the work.

Service panel

Panel upgrades

What's included: A full load calculation, a new panel and breakers sized to it, the permit paperwork, coordination with the utility on the service change, and a final test before the cover goes on.

Why it's planned first: A panel gets sized for the house you have plus what you're adding, not swapped one-for-one on a guess.

You'll want this if: the panel's still fed by fuses or a 100A service, breakers trip under normal use, or you're adding enough load (a garage, an EV charger, a whole floor) that the math doesn't work on what's there now.

Level 2 EV

EV charger installation

What's included: A dedicated circuit sized for the charger, a load check to confirm the panel can carry it without derating anything else, the charger mounted and wired, and a test charge before we leave.

Why it's planned first: A panel that looks like it has room on paper doesn't always have the real headroom once the math gets done.

You'll want this if: you've bought (or are about to buy) an EV and the garage has a regular outlet at best, or a previous install has always felt underpowered.

Fixture plan

Lighting design & install

What's included: A layout for the room, or the house, before fixtures go in: what's on dimmers, what's on its own switch, where recessed cans actually earn their spot instead of just following the framing. Then the wiring and the fixtures.

Why it's planned first: Switching that makes sense costs the same as switching that doesn't. The difference is whether anyone drew it out first.

You'll want this if: you're mid-remodel, a room is always either too dark or lit like an office, or you're finally replacing the builder-grade fixtures the house came with.

Diagnostic

Troubleshooting & repair

What's included: A methodical trace from the panel out: breaker, then circuit, then device, until we find the actual fault, not just the symptom. You get a plain explanation of what was wrong before we fix it.

Why it's planned first: Replacing parts until something works costs more than finding the fault does. We test before we swap anything.

You'll want this if: a breaker trips with no obvious pattern, an outlet's gone dead, or the flickering isn't the bulb.

Transfer switch

Generator hookups

What's included: A transfer switch wired to isolate your house from the utility line when the generator's running, sized and circuited so the panel knows which loads matter most during an outage.

Why it's planned first: Which circuits actually need power during an outage is a household decision, not a default. We work that out before anything's wired.

You'll want this if: you already own a generator (or are about to) and don't want to run extension cords through a window during a storm.

How we scope it

Every job on this page starts with a walk-through and a real number in writing before anything's scheduled, not a ballpark that turns into a surprise once the wall's open.
