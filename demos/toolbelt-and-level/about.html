One person, one truck, one level

I spent a long stretch doing finish carpentry for other people’s crews before I went out on my own. Cabinet doors that wouldn’t sit flush used to bother me more than they should have. Somewhere in there I started carrying a level for more than cabinets, and it turned into the way I check everything – a hung TV, a rehung door, a shelf that’s supposed to hold real weight.

Toolbelt & Level is one truck and whatever the day’s list calls for. I quote the job after I’ve actually looked at it, not off a description over the phone, and I try to clear the whole list in a single visit so you’re not setting aside a second Tuesday for the same handful of items.

There’s plenty I don’t touch. Anything that needs a permit, a gas line, or a licensed electrician gets referred to someone who is, and I’ll say so on the first call instead of after I’ve started. The rest – a wobbly shelf, a sticking window, a nine-item move-in list – is exactly what this is for.

How it runs, day to day

One person, start to finish

The person who looks at the job is the person who does it. No dispatched crew you’ve never met.

A window, not an all-day wait

You get a two-hour arrival window and a text when I’m on the way, not a vague “sometime Tuesday.”

Materials at cost

No markup games on hardware or parts. You see what was needed and what it cost.

Checked level before I leave

Every item gets a level, a tug, or a swing test, and we walk the list together before I pack up.
