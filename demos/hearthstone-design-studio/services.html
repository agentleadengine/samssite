Five services, one way of working.

Custom homes, additions, permit drawings, 3D visualization, and feasibility studies. Whatever stage you're at, here's what's actually included.

Custom home design

We start at the site, not a floor plan book: sun, slope, neighbors, and the view worth keeping. Concept sketches come first, then floor plans that get refined with you room by room until the house fits how your family actually lives.

When you need it: a new build on a lot you own or are considering, or a teardown-rebuild.

Concept sketches · Floor plans · Elevations

Additions & renovations

A new primary suite, a kitchen that finally opens to the yard, a second story added without the house looking like it grew a hat. We match roofline, proportion, and material to the house you already have, then draw the parts that change.

When you need it: the house doesn't fit the family anymore, or a room just doesn't work.

Roofline matching · Room additions · Full-floor renovations

Permit drawing sets

The drawings a builder pulls a permit with: plans, elevations, sections, and the notes an inspector actually looks for. Prepared to your town's requirements, not a generic template.

When you need it: a design is settled and it's time to file for a permit, or a builder needs a stamped set to bid from.

Stamped set · Plans & sections · Code notes

3D concept visualization

A simple 3D model built from the same drawings your builder will use, so you can walk through a new kitchen or see how an addition sits against the yard before anything is framed.

When you need it: you're choosing between a couple of layouts, or you want to see it before you commit to it.

3D massing · Walkthrough views · Material studies

Site feasibility studies

Before design money gets spent, we check what your lot actually allows: setbacks, coverage limits, height limits, and what a realistic footprint looks like. Sometimes the honest answer is smaller than what you had in mind, and it's cheaper to hear that now.

When you need it: you're considering a lot, or you're not sure what your property allows before you commit to a design.

Zoning check · Setbacks · Realistic footprint

What actually shapes a design.

Not a style you point to in a magazine. Four things we check on every project, custom home or small addition alike.

Sun & site

Where morning light lands, where the slope drains, what's worth keeping a view of.

Family flow

Where the shoes land, where the noise needs a door, where the kids actually play.

Neighborhood character

Roofline, setback, and scale that reads like it belongs on the block.

Honest budget

A design sized to what it will actually cost to build, not a number we soften until the bids come in.
