Started with a lot everyone said was too small.

I started this studio because most of the houses I was drawing for other firms were being designed for a style board, not for the family that would actually live there. A house that photographs well but never gets used the way it looks is a house that's failed at the one job that matters.

The part I care about most happens before a single pretty drawing gets made. A site visit, a walk through the house you're in now, a real conversation about where the mornings actually go sideways. Setbacks and zoning get checked early too, honestly, because a design nobody can permit is just an expensive sketch.

I work across Long Island, on new homes, additions, and the kind of renovation where a family has simply outgrown the house they love. If your lot only supports a smaller footprint than you had in mind, that's what I'll tell you, even before you've paid for a single drawing.

How a project actually runs.

First

Site walk & first sketch

We walk the lot or the house together, talk through what's not working, and leave with a rough concept sketch, not a sales pitch.

Then

Concept & floor plans

Two or three directions get sketched and refined with you into real floor plans and elevations, checked against setbacks as we go.

Next

3D model & permit drawings

A simple 3D model lets you walk through it before it's final. Once it's approved, it becomes a full permit-ready drawing set.

Last

Filed, and here for the questions

Drawings go to the town or straight to your builder. We stay reachable through the questions that come up once construction starts.
