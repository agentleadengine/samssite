Work

Sample projects showing how we approach different materials and roof types. Each one is a composite illustration — the kind of work we do, not a specific client's house.

Sample project

Cedar Shake Replacement

A full tear-off of an aging asphalt roof and replacement with premium cedar shake. The staggered course pattern gives the roof a natural, uneven texture that ages to silver-gray over a decade. Copper ridge cap and drip edge for a clean transition.

Sample project

Slate Roof with Copper Valleys

Natural slate installed over a fully replacement deck, with copper valley flashings hand-cut and folded on site. Slate is heavy — the deck needs to be rated for it, and the underlayment matters more than on any other roof type. We plan for both.

Sample project

Flat Roof Membrane System

TPO membrane installation on a low-slope commercial section. Seams are heat-welded, penetrations are flashed with matching membrane, and the drainage path runs to a copper-edged scupper. The parapet wall gets full membrane coverage to prevent water tracking behind the flashing.

Sample project

Storm Damage Assessment

After a windstorm lifted and cracked shingles across a residential roof, we did a full inspection and marked three damage zones: a missing shingle exposing the underlayment, a lifted tab that would fail next rain, and a hairline crack running across a lower course. Written report, then targeted repair — no full replacement needed.

Sample project

Copper Gutter Integration

Seamless copper gutters tied into a slate roof with a new drip edge flashing. The gutter profile is half-round, which carries water more efficiently than K-style and sheds debris better. Downspouts route to extensions that move water four feet from the foundation.

Sample project

Architectural Asphalt Replacement

A straightforward asphalt shingle replacement done correctly: ice and water shield at the eaves and valleys, synthetic underlayment across the deck, starter strip at the eave, and architectural shingles with the tab pattern aligned. Not glamorous, but the roof that most Long Island houses need — done right.
