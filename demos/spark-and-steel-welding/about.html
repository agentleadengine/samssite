One welder,
one truck, no shop.

No storefront, no dispatcher, no markup on parts you didn't need. Here's who shows up and how the whole thing actually runs.

The idea for Spark & Steel came from the same complaint, over and over: a gate or a trailer breaks, and the estimate to fix it costs more in hauling and shop time than the actual weld. So the shop moved into a truck instead of the other way around.

The whole operation is built to run like a shop on its own: a generator, a welder, a grinder, a cutting torch, and enough stock steel to fabricate small pieces without a supply run first. If it's steel and it's stuck where it broke, that's usually enough to fix it in one visit.

There's no showroom and no sales floor. You call, you talk to Deb, the person who's actually driving the truck, and if the job needs a real shop instead of a rig, you'll hear that on the phone instead of after a wasted trip.

How a call turns
into a repair

No account to set up and no ticket number. Here's exactly how it goes from phone call to finished weld.

01

Call or text

02

A rough idea of cost and whether it's a one-visit fix, before anyone drives anywhere.

03

Truck rolls

Deb shows up with the rig, looks at the actual steel, and quotes it for real before the torch comes out.

04

Fixed on the spot

Most jobs finish in the same visit. Cut, welded, ground smooth, and back in place.

Service area

Long Island, NY. The truck goes wherever the steel is: driveways, job sites, back lots, and yards.

How the truck runs

  • Every job is priced before the torch comes out. You'll hear a real number on-site, not a surprise on the invoice.
  • If it's not a truck job, you'll hear that on the phone. Some repairs genuinely need a shop, and we'll say so instead of taking the drive.
  • You watch the weld. No drop-off, no black box. It gets fixed in front of you.
