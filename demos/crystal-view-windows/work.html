Recent-looking work, drawn from how the jobs actually go.

Six sample projects. Scenes are illustrated, not photographed, and stand in for real jobs of each type.

Sample project

The two-story colonial

A whole-house visit on a tree-lined block: in and out on both floors, screens pulled and washed, ladder work on the upper panes where the ground allowed it. Four hours from the first ring to the last squeegee stroke.

Sample project

The storefront before opening

A recurring stop on a strip of small shops. We’re in and out before the lights go on inside, so the glass is clear before the first customer ever notices it was dirty.

Sample project

Screens that hadn’t come apart in years

Tracks packed with a decade of pollen and driveway dust. Every screen came out, got washed flat, and went back into a frame that finally closed the way it was built to.

Sample project

The sunroom after a hard pollen season

Angled glass on three sides, coated in a yellow film by late spring. Pole work from the inside and the outside, corner to corner, until the room stopped looking like it was behind a filter.

Sample project

New windows, still wearing the install

Paint flecks on the glass, a bead of stucco spray on the trim, sticker residue on every pane. A scraper job before it was ever a squeegee job, priced once we’d actually seen it.

Sample project

The walk-through before we pack up

The last five minutes of any visit: checking the glass at an angle, in whatever light is actually in the room, for the one streak a straight-on look would have missed.
