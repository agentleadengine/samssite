A small outfit, on purpose

Swift Drain stays small enough that the person who quotes a job over the phone is usually the one who shows up to do it.

Nick Farrell started Swift Drain after years riding along on somebody else's truck, learning which Long Island houses still have galvanized in the walls and which got upgraded in the 90s. He kept the company small on purpose: one or two vans, not a fleet, so the person who quotes a job over the phone is usually the one who shows up to do it.

The name is a promise more than a slogan. A slow drain shouldn't turn into an all-day wait, and a leak shouldn't turn into a guessing game about what it'll cost. Swift Drain runs on real arrival windows and one number, quoted before anything gets opened up.

Most calls are ordinary: a running toilet, a water heater that's given up, a drain that backs up at the worst possible time. Swift Drain treats the ordinary ones like they matter, because to the person standing in the kitchen, they do.
