One truck. No dispatcher.

Blue Ladder started because every property manager I worked for kept a running list of small stuff that never got fixed — not because it was hard, but because no contractor wanted to drive out for a two-hour job. I figured if I stocked the truck right and kept the jobs small, I could actually keep up with that list instead of watching it grow.

Most days start with a phone call and a rough list — a sticking door, a patch that needs feathering, a faucet somebody already bought. I’ll tell you honestly how long the whole thing looks like before anything gets booked, and if something needs a license I don’t have, I’ll say so instead of taking the job anyway and figuring it out on your wall.

There’s no office and no dispatcher — just the truck, the tools that live in it, and a phone that gets answered until seven most evenings. If you get voicemail, leave the town you’re in and what needs fixing, and you’ll hear back the same day.

What a normal call sounds like

You read off the list, I ask a few questions — what it’s made of, how bad it is, whether you’ve got the part already. We land on a morning or afternoon window that works for you, and I text when I’m on the way so you’re not staring out the window all day. Before I leave, we walk the finished list together — nothing gets marked done that you haven’t seen for yourself.
