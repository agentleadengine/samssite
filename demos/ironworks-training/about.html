How this
gym runs

No franchise, no front desk, no sales pitch. Here's who's running it and how the model actually works.

Dana started Ironworks out of a converted garage on the North Shore, watching the same pattern over and over: people quitting programs that never fit their week in the first place. So the model here stays deliberately plain. One coach, one plan, adjusted on a schedule instead of a whim.

There's no front desk and no sales pitch. You call, you talk to the person who will actually coach you, and if it's not a fit, you'll hear that on the phone instead of finding out three sessions in.

Training happens at a small studio space or at your own setup, whichever gets you training more often. The equipment is basic on purpose: barbells, plates, a few racks, and enough room to move well.

Four steps,
no mystery

Nobody gets handed a generic plan on day one. Here's exactly how the first month goes.

01

Phone call

Ten minutes. What you want, what hurts, what your week looks like, and whether this is a fit. If it's not, we'll say so.

02

Assessment session

We move through the basic patterns and find your starting loads. You leave having trained, not just having been measured.

03

Written plan

You get the plan in writing with sets, reps, and the reason each thing is on there. Ask questions. It's your training.

04

Train and reassess

One to three sessions a week, logged every time. Every six weeks we look at the numbers together and change what needs changing.

Service area

Long Island, NY. Sessions run at the training space or at your home setup, whichever gets you training more often.

How sessions run

  • Every session gets written down: weights, reps, and how it felt. It's your log, and you can ask to see it any time.
  • Plans change. Give us until 6pm the day before and we'll move your session instead of charging you for missing it.
  • The floor stays calm. No shouting, no chest-thumping, just the work.
