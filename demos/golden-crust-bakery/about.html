One counter, one kitchen

No second location, no delivery drivers, no franchise paperwork. Just the people who show up while it's still dark out.

Golden Crust is one counter and one kitchen, not a chain and not a franchise. Whoever picks up the phone in the afternoon was probably icing something an hour earlier. The bakery doesn't hold stock overnight, which means some days you'll catch everything and some days you'll catch what's left. Both are normal here.

Selling same-day only isn't a marketing line, it's just how the math works for a small kitchen. Freezing and reheating would let us bake less and sell longer, but that's not what the place is for. Dough gets mixed the night before, shaped and baked starting at four, and whatever doesn't sell by close doesn't come back out the next morning.

Cakes and trays are handled by phone, since the counter gets busy early and there's no online system pretending to know what's actually on the shelf right now. Everything is pickup, at the shop, serving Long Island, NY. If nobody answers when you call, leave your name, the date you need it, and a callback number. Calls get returned in the afternoon, once the ovens slow down.

How an order actually happens

Three steps, all of them by phone.

Call the bakery

Tell us what you need and roughly when. If nobody picks up, leave your name, the date, and a callback number.

We confirm the details

Size, flavor, headcount, what to write on it, whatever the order calls for. We call back in the afternoon lull to lock it in.

Pick up at the counter

Everything is pickup only, at the shop, at the time we agreed on when you called.
