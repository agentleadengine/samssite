The person on your equipment pad

One route, run the same way every week. Here's the story behind it, the towns it covers, and how a first month usually goes.

How it started

Clearwater started as one truck and a route sheet. Danny spent a few summers in high school working behind the counter at a pool supply store, answering the same questions again and again: why did the water turn green, why won't the pump prime, why does the filter pressure keep climbing. Enough of those turned into actual house calls that the house calls became the job.

The route still runs the way it started: the same person on your equipment pad every week, on the same day, texting you what they found instead of leaving you to guess. Chemicals are in the weekly rate, not billed as a surprise add-on, and if something needs a part, you hear the price before it gets ordered.

Most weeks are quiet — brush, vacuum, test, balance, done in twenty minutes, on to the next address. The interesting calls are the green ones: a pool that got away from someone during a vacation or a stretch of storms, and needs a few straight days of attention instead of one visit. Either way, you get a text when the truck pulls out of the driveway, not just an invoice at the end of the month.

What a first month usually looks like

The order things happen in, from the first call to the regular weekly rhythm.

1

You call

Tell us the pool size, the surface, and whether it's screened or open to trees. That's usually enough to quote the weekly rate right on the phone.

2

We walk the pad together

On the first visit we test the water, note anything that's worn, and lock in your regular service day.

3

The route runs itself

Same day, every week. You don't need to be home, and you don't need to leave anything out for us.

4

You hear from us

A text after every visit with the readings and anything worth watching. Repair prices come before the work, not after.
