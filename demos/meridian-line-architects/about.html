About Meridian Line

Most house projects start with a picture torn from somewhere else — a kitchen from a magazine, a porch from a neighbor's feed. Meridian Line starts differently: with the lot itself, which direction it faces, where the light lands in January versus July, and which rooms a family actually lives in versus the ones that just look nice in photos and stay empty.

The same person who walks the site draws the plans, sits with you while they change, and signs the permit set at the end. Nothing gets handed off to someone you haven't met partway through. Every set is drawn the way it actually gets built — framing, dimensions, and notes a contractor can work from without calling to ask what was meant.

It's a small studio, on purpose, working on houses across Long Island, NY: new builds, additions, and the drawings a permit office needs to sign off on either one. Not everything is a fit. If a project needs a bigger firm or a different kind of expertise, that comes up on the first call, not after a deposit.

How it works

No hand-offs, and nothing finalized before you've seen it.

You call

Tell us the town and what you're trying to do. A few minutes on the phone usually sorts out whether we're the right fit.

We visit the site

Measure, look at access and what's structurally in play, and talk through what's worth doing now versus later.

Drawings, reviewed together

Nothing gets finalized without you seeing it first. Changes happen here, on paper, before they'd cost anything to change in framing.

A set the permit office can approve
