The number doesn't change once it's written down.

Rapid Flat‑Rate Rooter runs on one rule: look at the job, say the number, do the job for that number. Here's how that actually plays out on a call.

Rapid Flat‑Rate Rooter started after one too many jobs where the number on the phone wasn't the number on the invoice. Nobody was necessarily trying to pull one over — “we'll know more once we're in there” just has a way of growing once the clock's the only thing keeping track.

So the whole business runs on one rule instead: look at the actual problem, say the number out loud, then do the job for that number. Not a floor. Not a starting point. The number.

That's still how a call goes today. Somebody describes what's happening, we ask enough to send a truck stocked for the real job, we look before we talk price, and once the number's said, it's written on the ticket — not something that gets revisited once a wall's open.

— Tommy Russo, Rapid Flat‑Rate Rooter

How a flat number actually gets set

  3. 3

    The number's written on the ticket before work starts. If the job turns out bigger once we're in there, you hear the new number before we touch anything else.
