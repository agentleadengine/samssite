Work

Sample projects that show how we approach the trade — the decisions, the care, and the finished result.

Sample project

1920s Kitchen Rewire

A galley kitchen with one shared 15-amp circuit running the fridge, microwave, and overhead light. We pulled two new dedicated circuits through the plaster ceiling above — no wall cuts, one access point in the cabinet above the fridge. Added GFCI protection at the counter outlets.

Sample project

Panel Replacement, 1930s Colonial

A 60-amp fuse box in a basement that had been added to three times. We ran a new 200-amp service entrance, replaced the panel, and separated the kitchen and bathroom onto dedicated circuits. The old fuse box cover stays as a wall piece — at the owner's request.

Sample project

Garage EV Charger, 1950s Cape

A detached garage with a 60-amp sub-panel already in place — barely. We verified the capacity, pulled a dedicated 40-amp circuit through existing conduit, and surface-mounted the charger outside the garage door. No trenching, no drywall.

Sample project

Living Room Lighting, Victorian

A 1890s parlor with 9-foot plaster ceilings and a single overhead fixture. We installed three shallow-depth recessed lights between the joists — 4-inch fixtures that fit the 6-inch joist bays without furring down the ceiling. New dimmer in the existing switch box, original plaster undisturbed.

Sample project

Generator Hookup, 1940s Ranch

A family that lost power for four days during a nor'easter. We installed a 6-circuit manual transfer switch next to the existing panel, an inlet box on the garage exterior, and identified the critical circuits: furnace, refrigerator, well pump, and kitchen lights. They plug in the portable generator and flip one switch.

Sample project

Knob-and-Tube Assessment, 1905 Farmhouse

A buyer's inspection flagged active knob-and-tube wiring in the attic. We mapped every visible run, identified which circuits were still live, and tested insulation integrity. Result: two runs needed replacement, three were safely retired and disconnected. The buyer knew exactly what they were dealing with before closing.
