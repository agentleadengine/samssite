About

Why this business exists, how it works, and where it goes.

The story.

I started Old House Rewire Co. after spending years doing general electrical work across Long Island and noticing the same thing on every pre-1950 call: someone had been in there before me, and they'd treated the house like it was built in 1995. Holes cut where they didn't need to be. Modern plastic boxes screwed into plaster that was never meant to hold them. Romex stapled to the outside of joists in an attic where it didn't belong.

Old houses aren't harder to wire. They're different. The framing is different, the wall material is different, the existing wiring is different. If you understand those differences — where the joists run, how thick the plaster is, what kind of wire is already in there — the work goes cleanly. If you don't, you end up cutting holes that didn't need to be cut and patching walls that didn't need to be touched.

So I narrowed the practice. Older homes on Long Island — that's the whole focus. Panel upgrades, EV chargers, lighting, troubleshooting, and generator hookups, done by someone who actually knows what's inside the walls of a 1920s colonial or a 1905 farmhouse. No crew shows up and treats your plaster like drywall.
