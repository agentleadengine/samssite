Everything we do to a chimney

Five jobs, no bundled packages. Each one is described here the way we'd explain it standing at your hearth: what's included, and what usually sends someone looking for this.

Chimney sweeping

What's included

  • Drop cloths down before anything gets touched
  • Brush and rod pass the full length of the flue, not just what's visible from the firebox
  • Glaze and creosote cleared off the liner walls
  • Firebox and smoke shelf swept out
  • Debris bagged and hauled away, not left for you to deal with

When you need it

  • You've burned a cord or more since the last cleaning
  • The flue smells sooty when it rains or the AC kicks on
  • Smoke drifts into the room instead of drawing up
  • It's simply been about a year since anyone looked

Inspections

What's included

  • Full visual check, top of the stack to the firebox
  • Camera pass down the flue looking for cracks, gaps, or blockage
  • Cap, crown, flashing, damper, and liner condition checked
  • A plain written rundown of what we found, photo included where it helps

When you need it

  • Buying or selling a house with a fireplace or insert
  • After a chimney fire, lightning strike, or bad storm
  • Before the first fire of the season, especially after it sat unused
  • Nobody can tell you if the liner's ever been checked

Cap & crown repair

What's included

  • Reseal or rebuild a cracked crown
  • Replace a rusted, bent, or missing cap
  • Add a spark arrestor screen where one's missing
  • Reflash where the crown meets the stack so water sheds instead of soaking in

When you need it

  • Water stains showing up on the ceiling near the chimney
  • You can see daylight, rust, or gaps at the top of the stack
  • Animals or leaves keep finding their way into the flue
  • Cracks in the crown are visible from the ground

Liner installs

What's included

  • Stainless liner sized correctly to the appliance it's serving
  • Insulated where the job calls for it
  • Clean connection at both the firebox and the cap
  • Draft tested once it's in, before we call it done

When you need it

  • Switching from oil to gas, or adding an insert
  • The old clay tile liner is cracked or crumbling
  • The chimney was never lined to begin with
  • An inspection turned up a liner that's failing

Masonry touch-ups

What's included

  • Repoint mortar joints that have gone chalky or crumbled out
  • Reset loose or spalling brick
  • Patch small cracks in the stack before they spread
  • Match the mortar color so the repair doesn't stand out

When you need it

  • Mortar joints look chalky or are missing between bricks
  • Bricks are flaking or popping off in chunks
  • A crack is visibly running down the outside of the stack
  • The chimney looks like it's leaning or pulling from the house

Not sure which one you need?

Tell us what you're seeing or hearing and we'll tell you plainly what it sounds like before anyone comes out.
