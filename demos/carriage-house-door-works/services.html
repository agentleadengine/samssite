Five ways we touch a door

Design and installation, the repairs that come up, and the maintenance that keeps a well-built door running the way it did on day one.

New door installation

We start with a design conversation, not a catalog. Panel layout, window row, stain or paint, and hardware finish all get decided against a photo of your actual house before anything is built.

The face is built in the shop to the opening we measured, not a stock size trimmed to fit. A full hardware set is included, and the crew that measured it does the install.

What’s included

Design conversation, template measure, custom-built face, full hardware set, installation and haul-away of the old door.

When you need it

A builder-grade door that never matched the house, a new opening on an addition, or you’re ready for something drawn on purpose.

Spring & cable service

Torsion springs and lift cables get matched to your door’s actual weight, not pulled off a generic rack. A spring wound for the wrong door either fights the opener or lets the door drop.

We check the drums and bearings while we’re in there, since a spring rarely fails on its own.

A wound torsion spring holds real force. If one has already let go, leave it alone. Call and describe what you’re seeing and we’ll tell you what’s safe to do until we arrive.

What’s included

Spring and cable replacement, balance check, drum and bearing inspection.

When you need it

The door feels heavy to lift, you heard a loud snap, or a cable looks frayed or off the drum.

Opener install & repair

Quiet belt-drive openers, smart controls if you want them, sized to the door instead of maxed out for no reason.

Repairs cover the motor, the rail and trolley, and the safety sensors that stop the door from closing on something in the way.

What’s included

Opener install or repair, safety sensor alignment, remote and keypad setup.

When you need it

The opener is loud, slow or not responding, or a new door needs an opener sized to match.

Annual tune-up

Hinges and rollers get lubricated, hardware gets tightened, and the balance and travel limits get checked against how the door is actually moving now, not how it moved on install day.

A door that’s gotten a little louder or slower over the seasons is usually a maintenance issue, not a replacement one.

What’s included

Full hardware inspection, lubrication, balance and travel-limit adjustment, weatherseal check.

When you need it

Once a year is plenty, or any time the door sounds different than it used to.

Off-track service

A slipped roller or a bent track can jump a door off its rails. It looks alarming, but most of the time it isn’t dangerous if you stop and leave it alone.

Don’t force the door or try to muscle it back into the track yourself, especially if the spring is still under tension. Call and describe what you’re seeing, and we’ll tell you what’s safe to do until we get there.

What’s included

Track realignment or replacement, roller replacement, a full safety check before we leave.

When you need it

The door is stuck partway, sitting crooked in the opening, or has visibly come off the track.

Not sure which one you need?

Call anyway. Tell us what the door is doing and we’ll sort out which service actually applies.
