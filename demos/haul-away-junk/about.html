One truck. No dispatcher. No surprises.

Here's how Haul Away actually runs, from the first call to where everything ends up.

Haul Away started with one truck and a favor: a neighbor needed a garage cleared before a yard sale, and it turned into a full Saturday of hauling. A few more calls came that same week, then a few more after that.

Today it's still one truck doing most of the work. Whoever answers the phone is usually the one who shows up to load it, which means the price you hear on the call is the price you get at the curb. No dispatcher relaying details, no surprise line items once the truck is full.

Where things end up matters too. Metal goes to the scrap yard, furniture that still has life in it goes to donation, and the rest gets disposed of the right way, not just the fastest way. It stays a small operation on purpose. Smaller means the person who quoted the job is the person who did it.

How a pickup actually goes

Same four steps whether it's one couch or a full basement.

1

Call or text a photo

A picture of the pile gets you a real number instead of a vague range over the phone.

2

Price up front

Before anything moves, including any dump fees for heavy material.

3

Load and sweep

You point, we carry. Floors get swept and doorways get checked before we leave.

4

Sorted the right way

Metal to the scrap yard, usable furniture to donation, the rest disposed of properly.

Where we run

Long Island, NY. Residential driveways, walk-up apartments, small offices, and job sites all get the same truck and the same crew. Tight streets and low-clearance parking are worth mentioning on the call so we bring the right truck.
