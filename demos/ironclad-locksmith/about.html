The person who answers is the person who shows up.

Ironclad started because too many locksmith calls on Long Island go through a dispatch center that has no idea what the job actually costs. Danny Marsh picks up the phone here, and the price you hear is based on the lock and the hour — not a guess that changes once the truck shows up.

Most calls are the same handful of things: a car door, a house door, a rekey after moving in, a key that snapped off in the cylinder. The work itself isn't complicated. Being straight about the cost and showing up when you say you will is the part that's easy to get wrong, so that's the part Ironclad tries hardest to get right.

If the lock is something outside the usual list, say so on the phone. It's a faster answer than driving out to find out.

— Danny Marsh, Ironclad Locksmith

Where we go

Long Island, NY, day or night. Not sure a specific spot is covered? Call and ask — it takes ten seconds.

How the call works

You describe the lock, you hear a price, and only then does anyone get in a truck. If the job changes once we're there — a cylinder more damaged than it sounded on the phone — you hear about it before it's done, not on the invoice after.

The phone doesn't go to voicemail. Not at 3am, not on Christmas.

Overnight and holiday work carries an after-hours rate, quoted before you agree to it.

Ready when you are.

Same person, same price-first approach, every call.
