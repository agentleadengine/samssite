What a repair call actually looks like

Blowing warm since Tuesday. We check refrigerant charge, capacitor and airflow, in that order, because that's usually where the fault sits. On this call the capacitor had swollen and lost its charge: a small part causing a system-wide symptom.

We swapped it, watched the gauge settle back into the steady zone, and left the old one on the counter so you can see exactly what failed.

This is the shape of most repair calls: a specific part, a specific reading, a specific fix. Not a guess and a number.

Four things we do every job

HVAC has a reputation for surprise numbers and hard upsells. This is how we stay out of that, in the order it actually happens.

01

We measure before we quote

Square footage, insulation, window load, duct condition. Guessing the size is how a house ends up clammy in August.

02

One flat number, in writing

You get the price before we start, parts and labor both. Find something else once we're in the wall, we stop and ask first.

03

You see the part that failed

Swollen capacitor, seized blower motor, a contactor burned black; it goes on the counter. Anything we can't pull out, like a cracked heat exchanger, you get the camera photo and the CO reading.

04

Repair first when it makes sense

A ten-year-old system with one dead part usually gets fixed, not replaced. We'll tell you plainly when it's throwing good money after bad.

Ready when the system isn't.

Tell us what it's doing and roughly how old it is. That's usually enough to know what to load on the truck.
