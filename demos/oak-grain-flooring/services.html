Wood floors, start to finish.

Install, sand, stain, finish, and repair. No carpet, no tile, no laminate. If it's a wood floor in a Long Island house, it's ours to work on.

New hardwood installation

Material sits in the house first so it can settle to your humidity. We check the subfloor with a straightedge and a moisture meter, flatten the high and low spots, then rack the boards for grain and length before one gets nailed down.

When you need it: new construction, an addition, or swapping carpet or tile for real wood.

Solid & engineered · Nail-down · Glue assist

Sanding and refinishing

Three passes with the big machine, an edger into the perimeter, then a buffer to blend the two so there's no halo along the walls. Old finish, pet stains, and cupping get dealt with before any color goes down.

When you need it: the finish is worn through, the wood is gray or blackened, or scratches and old stains won't come out with a recoat.

Bare wood · Edges blended · Two coats minimum

Stain samples on your floor

Color reads differently on red oak than it does on white oak, and differently again under your lights. We put three or four samples right on your floor and you pick in the room where it has to live.

When you need it: any refinish or new install where the color isn't chosen yet, or a store chip has never once matched the room.

Natural · Warm · Neutral · Dark

Board repair and replacement

Water damage under a dishwasher, a cracked board by the slider, a gap left behind after a wall came out. We weave new boards into the existing floor by species, width, and grain direction so the repair disappears once it's sanded and finished.

When you need it: a leak, a crack, or a patch left behind by other work in the house.

Weave in · Patch · Water damage

Stairs, treads, and risers

Carpet comes off, staples come out, and treads get capped or replaced to match the floor. Risers painted or stained, nosing profiled, and the run finished so the stairs and the hallway read as one floor.

When you need it: carpeted stairs that no longer match a new or refinished floor, or treads worn through at the nose.

Tread caps · Risers · Nosing

Screen and recoat

If the finish is worn but the wood underneath it is still clean, a full sanding isn't necessary. We abrade the old coat, clean it down, and lay a fresh coat of poly over what's already there.

When you need it: the finish looks dull or scuffed but the wood itself isn't gray, cupped, or stained through, usually every few years between full refinishes.

One day · Low dust · Lower cost

Every species takes stain its own way.

Red oak pulls warm, almost pink. White oak stays cooler and can take gray without turning muddy. Maple fights stain and usually looks best clear. We'll tell you what your wood will actually do before you commit to a color.

Red oakWarm, open grain
White oakCooler, tighter grain
WalnutDeep brown, softer
HickoryHigh contrast, hard
MaplePale, best clear
AshLight, strong grain
