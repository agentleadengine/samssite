Wood floors, start to finish.

Install, sand, stain, seal, and repair. No carpet, no tile, no laminate — if it's a wood floor in a Long Island house, it's the work. Six services, what each one includes, and when it's the one you need.

New hardwood installation

Material sits in the house first so it can settle to your humidity. We check the subfloor with a straightedge and a moisture meter, flatten the high and low spots, then rack the boards for grain and length before one gets nailed down.

When you need it: new construction, an addition, or replacing something that was never wood to begin with.

Solid & engineeredNail-downGlue assist

Sanding and refinishing

Three passes with the big machine, an edger into the perimeter, then a buffer to blend the two so there's no halo along the walls. Old finish, pet stains, and cupping get dealt with before any color goes down.

When you need it: the wood is sound but the finish is shot — deep scratches, gray or black stains, or a color you're ready to change.

Bare woodEdges blendedTwo coats minimum

Stain samples on your floor

Color reads differently on red oak than on white oak, and differently again under your lights. We put three or four samples right on your floor and you pick in the room where it has to live.

When you need it: any time you're refinishing or installing and haven't settled on a color yet.

NaturalWarmNeutralDark

Board repair and replacement

Water damage under a dishwasher, a cracked board by the slider, a gap left behind after a wall came out. We weave new boards into the existing floor so the repair disappears once it's sanded and finished.

When you need it: a stain, a crack, or a gap you'd rather not stare at every day.

Weave inPatchWater damage

Stairs, treads and risers

Carpet comes off, staples come out, and treads get capped or replaced to match the floor. Risers painted or stained, nosing profiled, and the run finished so the stairs and the hallway read as one floor.

When you need it: carpet coming off the stairs, or a staircase that stopped matching a newly finished hallway.

Tread capsRisersNosing

Screen and recoat

If the finish is worn but the wood under it is still clean, you don't need a full sanding. We abrade the old coat, clean it down, and lay a fresh coat of poly. One day of work, and a fraction of a refinish.

When you need it: a fingernail test shows clean wood under a finish that's just gone dull.

One dayLow dustLower cost

Every species takes stain its own way.

Red oak pulls warm and a little pink. White oak stays cooler and can take gray without going muddy. Maple fights stain and usually looks best clear. We'll tell you what your wood will actually do before you commit to a color.

Red oakWarm, open grain
White oakCooler, tighter grain
WalnutDeep brown, softer
HickoryHigh contrast, hard
MaplePale, best clear
AshLight, strong grain
