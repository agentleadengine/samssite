Started with a sander, not a plan.

I started out helping install floors for another company right out of high school, mostly carrying material and running the shop vacuum. The big sander was the machine nobody wanted to hand a new guy, so naturally it's the one I got good at first. Oak & Grain happened because I liked both halves of the job, the rough carpentry of getting a floor in and the slower work of bringing an old one back, and most companies around here only do one.

The part I care about most happens before anyone sees it. A straightedge across the boards, a moisture meter in a few spots, and I know whether we're looking at a recoat or a full sand before I say a number out loud. Stain gets tested on your actual floor, in your actual light, because a chip in a store never looks quite the same once it's down.

I work across Long Island, in houses people are living in and houses that are empty before a move-in, plus a few small commercial spaces after hours. Estimates are free and come in writing. If your floor only needs a screen and recoat, that's what I'll tell you, even though a full sanding costs more.

How a job actually runs.

First

Walk the floor

We check thickness, cupping, gaps, and old repairs, then say honestly whether it needs a full sand or only a recoat. The written estimate follows the same day or the next.

Then

Prep and sand

Furniture out, doorways sealed, vents covered. Rough cut, medium, then fine, with the edger and buffer working the perimeter so nothing shows a line.

Next

Stain and seal

Samples get approved first. Stain goes on and comes off by hand, then sealer, then the poly coats with a light abrasive pass in between.

Last

Cure and move back

Socks on the floor after a day, furniture back after a few days, rugs last. You get the exact schedule for your finish, and we walk the floor together at the end.
