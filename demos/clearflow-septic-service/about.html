Why ClearFlow runs the way it does.

A few paragraphs from Frank Doyle, who started it.

I spent years pumping tanks for someone else before I started ClearFlow. The work itself was never the problem — it's honest work, and somebody has to do it right. What got to me was how the industry treated people: vague arrival windows, no explanation of what was actually wrong, and upsells pitched at the worst possible moment, usually right when water's backing up into someone's basement.

ClearFlow runs on two tracks. Most of what we do is scheduled — pumping and inspections booked ahead, so nobody's guessing when their tank was last serviced. The rest is the call that can't wait: a backup at 6 a.m., a yard that's suddenly wet where it shouldn't be. Both get the same standard. We don't sell urgency that isn't there, and we don't downplay urgency that is.

Here's how it actually works: you call or text, we ask a few real questions — tank size if you know it, roughly when it was last pumped — and give you an appointment window we intend to keep. On site, we tell you what we're doing and what we find, in plain language, before we do anything beyond the original ask. The yard gets left the way we found it.
