Five things, one honest number each.

Everything below is quoted the same way: tell us what's going on, we ask a few questions, and you get a flat price before anything gets scheduled. No hourly clock running while we figure it out.

If what you need isn't listed here, call anyway — most jobs are some combination of these five.

Drain cleaning

What's included: cable clearing of a slow or blocked drain — kitchen, bathroom, laundry, or a floor drain — plus a check of what's downstream so the same clog doesn't come back in a month.

When you need it: water backing up in the tub when the washer runs, a sink that drains like it's thinking it over, or a gurgle from one drain when another fixture runs.

Water heater repair & install

What's included: repair on tanks and tankless units — pilot, thermostat, element, or valve — or a full swap when the tank's actually done, sized to the house rather than just to the old spot.

When you need it: no hot water, hot water that runs out fast, or a tank that's rusting at the seams or humming louder than it used to.

Leak detection & repair

What's included: tracking down a leak before it's obvious — under a slab, inside a wall, behind a fixture — then the repair itself, done once we actually know what we're dealing with.

When you need it: a water bill that jumped for no clear reason, a damp spot on a ceiling or floor, or a water meter that keeps spinning with everything in the house shut off.

Fixture install

What's included: faucets, sinks, toilets, tubs, and shower valves — swapped, upgraded, or moved, set level and sealed right the first time.

When you need it: a fixture you already bought that needs hanging right, an old one that's past fixing, or a remodel where the rough plumbing needs to match a new layout.

Sewer line service

What's included: clearing a blockage in the main line, a camera look at what's actually going on underground, and a repair plan once we know what we're dealing with.

When you need it: more than one drain backing up at the same time, a slow toilet that isn't the toilet's fault, or a smell from the yard that shouldn't be there.

How the number gets set

Describe what's happening when you call. We ask a few questions, then give you a flat price before anything gets scheduled — that's the number on the invoice. If we find something different once the job's actually open, we stop and confirm the new number with you before going further. No hourly clock, no surprise line items.

Not sure which one fits?

That's fine — most people call without knowing the trade name for what's wrong. Describe what you're seeing and we'll sort out which of the five it is.
