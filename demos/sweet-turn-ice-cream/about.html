The shop behind the window

Small batches, a short flavor board, and a walk-up window that opens the same time every day it's open.

Dana started churning ice cream in a kitchen that wasn't much bigger than the machine itself, and the shop still runs on the same idea: cook the base, cut the fruit, churn it in small batches, and stop when it's gone. Nothing sits in a walk-in freezer waiting for a slow week.

Most mornings start with a pot on the stove and end with a window full of people deciding between two scoops or three. The board changes with what came in that week — strawberries when they're actually in season, peppermint in December, and the same vanilla base every day because some things don't need reinventing.

Cakes and trays get worked in around the walk-up window, not instead of it, which is why cake orders need a couple of days' notice. If the answer on a busy Saturday is “we're a little behind today,” it's because someone's actually back there cutting fruit, not because a truck is late.

How the shop works

The window and the kitchen share one small space, so this is what keeps it moving.

Walk up for scoops

No call ahead for cones, sundaes or shakes. First come, first served at the window.

Call ahead for cakes

Two days' notice for most sizes. Tell us the flavors and what to write on top.

Trays are pickup only

We don't deliver, but we'll pack a tray or sundae bar cold enough to survive the ride.

We hold orders until closing

Can't make it until after work? A finished cake can wait for you in the freezer.

Split flavors, any cake

Two flavors on one cake is the default here, not a special request.
