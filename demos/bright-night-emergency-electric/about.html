The phone gets answered by the person who’s coming

Bright Night Emergency Electric is a small Long Island outfit built around one idea: the gap between “no power” and “someone’s here” shouldn’t be a voicemail.

Russ Calder ran service calls for a bigger electrical outfit for years, and the ones that stuck with him were always the same: someone calling at midnight with no power, getting an answering service that promised a callback by nine. By the time anyone showed up, the milk had gone warm and the kids had slept in coats. The gap wasn’t the electrical work. It was the phone.

Bright Night runs on one rule: the emergency line gets answered by a person, every time, day or night. If it’s actually urgent, a truck moves. If it can hold until morning, Russ says so honestly and books the first real appointment instead of treating a normal problem like a 2am emergency.

The daytime work is what keeps the night line possible — panel upgrades, EV chargers, lighting, generator hookups, all booked like any normal electrician. And when a night call turns out to be a symptom of something bigger, like a panel that’s simply worn out, that’s the conversation for daylight, not 2am.

Service area

Bright Night runs on Long Island, NY — houses, garages, and small storefronts that lose power at the worst possible time. Not sure you’re in range? Call and ask; it takes ten seconds.
