Springs, cables, openers, and the door around them

Most calls start with a spring or a cable. Here's what's actually involved when we fix it, plus the other jobs we handle when it's not an emergency.

New door installation

When the old door's past patching, rotted, storm-damaged, or you're just done fighting it, we pull it and hang a new insulated steel door balanced to your opening. Springs, cables and track get sized to the new door's actual weight, not left over from the one that came off.

Worth a look when

Panels are cracked or delaminating, the door's noticeably heavier or lighter to lift by hand than it used to be, or storm damage bent something that won't tap back straight.

Good to know

This one's scheduled, not same-day. We'll walk the opening first and give you a real number before anything's ordered.

Spring & cable repair

A torsion spring that's let go, or a cable that's frayed off the drum, is the single most common reason a door stops moving. We match the spring to your door's exact weight and wind it to the right tension, not a close-enough stand-in, and replace both springs as a pair when the original set is worn so they age together.

Signs it's this

A loud bang from the garage, the door suddenly feels twice as heavy to lift by hand, a visible gap in the coil above the door, or a cable hanging loose off its drum.

Good to know

This is the one we treat as an emergency. Most calls get a same-day window.

Opener install & repair

Grinding, a remote that's stopped answering, or an opener that's just worn out. We install belt and chain-drive openers, reset the force and travel limits so the door stops instead of straining, and program remotes and keypads to the new unit.

Signs it's this

The opener runs but the door doesn't move, the door reverses partway on its own, the remote works some days and not others, or the motor's noticeably louder than it used to be.

Good to know

A repair's often same-day. A full install usually gets scheduled a day or two out so we bring the right unit.

Annual tune-up

Rollers, hinges and track get lubricated, hardware gets torqued back down, and the spring's tension gets checked before it's the reason for a call in February. Twenty minutes, once a year, catches most of what turns into an emergency later.

Good timing for this

The door's gotten louder over the year, you can't remember the last time anyone looked at it, or it's right before winter, when cold makes worn parts fail faster.

Good to know

Scheduled, not urgent. We'll never talk you into one on an emergency call just because we're already there.

Emergency off-track service

A door that's jumped its track usually means a roller's broken or the track's been knocked out of line, not that the whole door needs replacing. We reset the door on its track, straighten or swap the bent section, and check that it's not fighting itself on the way back down.

Don't force it if

The door's sitting crooked in the opening, you can see daylight between a roller and the track, it stopped partway and won't go up or down, or something got backed into it.

Good to know

Leave it where it stopped. Most off-track calls get the same-day window.

Not sure which one you need?

Call anyway. Tell us what the door's doing and we'll tell you what it is, no charge for the diagnosis over the phone.
