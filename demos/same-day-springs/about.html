The voice on the phone is the one who shows up.

Pete Salerno ran service calls before he ever touched a garage door: trucks, generators, the kind of work where you're stuck until someone shows up. What stuck with him was how much a bad day gets worse when nobody tells you what's actually wrong, or when. A broken spring isn't complicated. Most of the wait is just the wait.

Same-Day Springs runs on one idea: the person you talk to on the phone is the person who shows up, and they already have a rough idea what's wrong before the truck leaves. You're not explaining the problem twice, once to a scheduler and again to whoever actually walks up your driveway.

The job itself stays simple. Pull the broken parts, put the right ones back matched to your door, and test it in front of you before anyone leaves. No stand-in spring that's close enough, and no coming back next week for the part that wasn't on the truck.

Same-day for the emergencies, scheduled for the rest

A snapped spring or a door stuck off its track jumps the line. A new door install or a tune-up gets booked a day or two out, on purpose, so the person doing it isn't rushing between two different kinds of job. If a call ahead of yours runs long, you hear about it before your window passes, not after it.
