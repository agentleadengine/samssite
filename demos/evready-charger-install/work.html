What it actually looks like when it's done

No client names and no addresses — six setups that look like what actually comes through the phone, drawn in the shop's own style rather than photographed.

Sample project

Level 2 charger, attached garage

The panel had headroom once we ran the numbers against the range and the dryer. One new 240-volt circuit through the attic to a garage wall unit, on its own breaker, sharing nothing.

Sample project

Charger for a detached garage, 90 feet out

The panel was on the house; the garage was 90 feet across the yard. We ran conduit underground instead of stringing anything overhead or through an extension cord, so the run's protected and permitted.

Sample project

100-amp service to 200, to make room

A 100-amp panel with two open slots left, already carrying central air and a finished basement. We swapped in a 200-amp service, moved every circuit over without leaving the house dead overnight, and landed the new charger breaker clearly labeled.

Sample project

Driveway and soffit lighting, same visit as the charger

Booked alongside a charger install: soffit lighting across the front of the house and two path lights along the driveway, on the same trip so the ladder and the trench only came out once.

Sample project

Whole-house transfer switch, planned ahead

Installed before the next outage, not during one: a transfer switch wired for the fridge, the sump pump, a handful of circuits, and the charger, so a portable generator can carry what actually matters.

Sample project

A charger that kept stopping mid-session

The charger would start, then quit about ten minutes in. Turned out to be a loose neutral connection at the junction box, not the charger itself — a clamp meter on the circuit found the voltage drop in about ten minutes.

Call and describe it. Most of the time we can tell you what it takes before anyone leaves for your house.
